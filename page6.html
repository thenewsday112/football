#37. Uganda: Bishop Kagodo, Kafeero Rally Kyagwe Ahead of Masaza Cup Final

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Bishop of Mukono Diocese, the Rev Enos Kitto Kagodo, and Assistant Inspector General of Police (AIGP) Moses Kafeero have rallied Kyagwe players ahead of their Buganda Masaza Cup final against Gomba on Saturday, October 31, 2026.

The two visited the team's training camp at St Joseph's Secondary School playground in Naggalama, Mukono District, where they watched the players train as preparations intensified for the decisive encounter.

Kagodo commended the team for raising Kyagwe's profile and bringing joy to its supporters, urging the players to approach the final with confidence, determination and a winning mentality.

He encouraged them to remain focused on scoring goals but cautioned against complacency, warning that overconfidence could undermine their performance on the pitch.

The bishop also prayed for God's guidance as Kyagwe seeks to win the coveted title.

Kafeero challenged the players to demonstrate courage and draw inspiration from the 30 days they had spent in residential training ahead of the final.

He said the team's performances had helped unite people across Kyagwe, noting that supporters from different political camps had come together to celebrate its victories.

Meanwhile, several companies and individuals have pledged Shs2 million for every goal Kyagwe scores in the final, alongside other prizes intended to motivate the players to deliver a strong performance.

The incentives come as the team prepares for another attempt at winning the annual football competition organised by the Buganda Kingdom.

Kyagwe will be making its second appearance in the final in three years, having first reached the decisive stage in 2024 before losing to Buddu.

The team will now seek to turn its latest final appearance into a historic triumph when it faces Gomba on Saturday.

For Kyagwe, victory would cap a campaign that has drawn supporters together and raised expectations across the county. Gomba, however, stands between the team and the coveted Masaza Cup crown.

#329. El Lens castiga a sus aficionados

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

El Racing Club de Lens francés castiga a sus aficionados para el derbi contra el Lille, que se disputará este viernes, tras los sucesos ocurridos el 21 de octubre en Brujas durante la tercera jornada de la Champions League, según informó este miércoles en un comunicado.

El Lens anunció que no pondrá a la venta las entradas del sector visitante, por lo que sus seguidores no podrán acudir al encuentro contra el Lille en el Stade Pierre-Mauroy, ubicado en la localidad de Villeneuve-d'Ascq.

Esta medida llega como castigo tras los incidentes de Brujas, donde, según explica el club, "los aficionados decidieron de forma colectiva lanzar fuegos artificiales", hasta 132, como recoge el informe de la UEFA.

"Este uso generalizado se produjo a pesar de que el RC Lens estaba bajo una estricta suspensión condicional por el cierre de su sección visitante en la Champions League, lo que ponía a todos sus seguidores en riesgo de sufrir nuevas sanciones", informó.

Asimismo el club francés denuncia que los actos cometidos en Brujas constituyen "pirotecnia peligrosa y hostil", así como el lanzamiento de proyectiles lanzados hacia el terreno de juego y los aficionados, incluidos los seguidores discapacitados del club.

La UEFA, que ya había adoptado una sanción en suspenso por incidentes semejantes en el encuentro ante el Slavia Praga en septiembre, prohibió el martes al Lens vender entradas a sus aficionados para los próximos dos partidos de la Champions contra el Leipzig y el Liverpool.

"Se ha cruzado una línea roja. Esta línea no era implícita. Había sido definida conjuntamente por el club y los grupos de aficionados tras las conversaciones mantenidas la temporada pasada, a raíz de incidentes anteriores en Metz, Lille, Londres y Reims. Los compromisos asumidos entonces se basaban en un principio sencillo: la seguridad de las personas y de quienes participan en el juego nunca debe ponerse en peligro", explicó el Lens, que perdió por 3-0 en Brujas.

Por ello y como medida estricta, el club anunció que no abrirá la grada visitante ante el Lille, ya que el club consideró que se ha "ignorado" el sentido de responsabilidad que todos debieron prometer y con la que pretende "castigar y prevenir".

"El club reitera con la mayor firmeza que el acceso al estadio está prohibido con el rostro cubierto por pasamontañas. Asimismo anuncia que, en adelante, siempre que las circunstancias lo permitan, las denuncias formales se presentarán sistemáticamente contra personas concretas en lugar de contra personas anónimas", termina el comunicado.

#160. Wrexham fined for fielding ineligible players

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Callum O'Hare (left) and Issa Kabore (right) joined Wrexham on permanent deals during the 2026 summer transfer window

Wrexham have been fined Â£75,000 - of which Â£25,000 is suspended - by the English Football League after two incidents of fielding ineligible players this season.

The EFL ruled that Issa Kabore and Callum O'Hare were ineligible to feature in Wrexham's games against Birmingham and Millwall, respectively.

Kabore came off the bench in Wrexham's 2-1 defeat by Birmingham City on 28 August, despite his Home Office 'right to work' documents not being correctly uploaded.

O'Hare, having joined from Sheffield United the day before, was an unused substitute in Wrexham's 3-0 win at Millwall on 2 September, though his documentation was not uploaded in time.

A club statement read: "We can confirm that Wrexham AFC has reached an agreed decision with the EFL concerning two breaches relating to player eligibility."

Burkina Faso international Kabore spent last season on loan with Wrexham but made his move from Manchester City permanent in the summer.

He returned to the Racecourse Ground on the eve of the contest with Birmingham, signing a three-year deal with the north Wales side.

The 25-year-old has featured 10 times in the Championship so far this season.

O'Hare also put pen to paper on a three-year contract with Wrexham in the summer after opting to leave Sheffield United.

Wrexham - currently 17th in the second tier after 12 games - are understood to be actively improving measures at the club to avoid future issues.

They are thought to be hiring a club secretary in addition to improving administrative training.

A club official told BBC Sport Wales that the incidents involving Kabore and O'Hare were not the fault of boss Phil Parkinson, the club's coaching staff or new sporting director Steve Nickson - who was not officially appointed to his role until 7 September.

The Wrexham statement added: "The club is taking the appropriate steps to prevent such issues occurring in the future."

El mandatario confirmó que el encuentro estará financiado por el sector privado y servirá como escaparate internacional antes de la Copa África 2027, que Kenia coorganizará junto a Uganda y Tanzania.

"Damas y caballeros, en enero de 2027 inauguraremos este estadio con un partido histórico entre el FC Bayern y el Ajax", dijo Ruto en redes sociales.

Ambos clubes confirmaron de forma independiente su participación en el encuentro inaugural, que aprovechará el parón invernal de la Bundesliga y la Eredivisie.

"Un partido especial para arrancar el nuevo año: el FC Bayern viajará a Nairobi el 5 de enero de 2027 y se enfrentará allí al Ajax de Ámsterdam con motivo de la inauguración ceremonial del nuevo estadio nacional", publicó el club alemán en la red social X.

"Hay una poesía silenciosa en este cruce. Raila estudió en Alemania y ahora un gran club alemán vendrá a jugar a la casa que lleva su nombre", afirmó el jefe de Estado keniano.

"Esto es más que la apertura de un estadio. Es una declaración de quiénes somos y, lo que es más importante, de en quién nos estamos convirtiendo", concluyó Ruto, al remarcar que la infraestructura mantendrá un modelo comercial autosostenible mediante alianzas corporativas.

Raila Odinga (1945–2025) fue una de las figuras más determinantes e influyentes de la historia política de Kenia. Más conocido como 'Baba', fue primer ministro entre 2008 y 2013, y pasó casi nueve años encarcelado sin juicio por su activismo en favor de la democracia multipartidista.

#41. Howe, Nagelsmann, Pochettino: can anyone end Manchester United’s doom loop?

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Even an elite coach would surely struggle at Old Trafford amid the executive chaos and with an unfit, uneven squad

In January 2014 the 13th series of Celebrity Big Brother aired in the UK, the show so popular that its run time was extended by five days before a public vote anointed Jim Davidson the winner. Then, two years later when Brexit happened, people wondered where their tolerant country had gone, lamenting the ephemeral spirit of London 2012 as though the 2011 riots were some kind of fever dream, not a desperate response to decades of structural and institutional racism exacerbated by the cruelty of unnecessary austerity.

The truth is that if security is quickly upended, it was built on quicksand to begin with. Which brings us to Manchester United, easing effortlessly from an uplifting third-place finish to some of the worst football in their history.

It is entirely possible that Alex Ferguson made a deal with the devil to the effect that United would be great until he retired and hilariously dreadful thereafter. But assuming not, his l’état, c’est moi mentality was busy setting up the post-him fall even at the height of the success – itself a consequence of what had gone before.

A day after the Munich air disaster, Louis Edwards – later a seller of condemned meat to schools – joined the board, eventually assuming control thanks to a dodgy rights issue. He bequeathed the club to Martin – his substellar son – who floated it for personal gain, turning an institution that sought to win, entertain and unify into a business legally compelled to return value to shareholders.

This left United vulnerable to acquisition by anyone and, when Ferguson fell out with two directors – a horse, a horse, his kingdom for a horse’s ejaculate – they sold to the Glazers, who loaded the purchase price and accordant interest obligation on to the club, with government and football authorities waving through a move since outlawed. Ferguson, meanwhile, must have feared exactly what the takeover would entail and known that without his support the banks might not have lent the money, while the chief executive David Gill admitted opposition to it but stayed in his role anyway, and so the pillaging began.

The English game then exploded, with investment in United left available only to those of wealth so extreme it necessarily casts aspersions over their character – such as arch polluter and anti‑immigration zealot, Jim Ratcliffe. Proclaiming inaccurate and divisive rhetoric contrary to the inclusive, cosmopolitan spirit of Manchester and United, jobs were culled and fights picked with fans, Old Trafford’s most desirable seats socially cleansed so the club could sell them to day-trippers for astronomical prices, with longstanding supporters banned after trumped-up charges of touting – a deeply principled stance the club fails to apply to itself.

Not untypically for a billionaire, Ratcliffe seemingly believes that correct opinions reflect exchequer not expertise, giving himself final say on footballing decisions. And rather than the “best in class” executives he promised, his United operate according to what political analysts call the “Boris principle”, no‑marks in thrall to their own self‑image given jobs for which they lack the ability and character, the consequent chaos being effective sabotage.

So the patterns continue: Champions League qualification is missed, insufficient funds are released; a new unsuitable manager spends heavily on players below requisite standard; Champions League qualification is achieved via unsustainably reactive style; the manager is denied necessary funds amounting to less than the bounty of Champions League qualification; the manager imposes a proactive style without the necessary materials; the manager is belatedly fired for a fee far exceeding that saved by cutting jobs; a new unsuitable manager, with entirely different requirements to his predecessor, is appointed; executive accountability is absent.

It is thus no surprise that Michael Carrick remains unfired, despite exuding the vibe of a man who’s mentally fired himself. Perhaps it’s a stay of execution, but more likely there’s bizarre hope of a turnaround, partly to save paying him off, partly because the relevant executives can’t achieve consensus on a replacement, and partly because surely even Omar Berrada and Jason Wilcox accept that, following another catastrophe, after dismissing Carrick they are honour-bound to dismiss themselves.

Except they and United don’t work like that.

So, just as they failed Carrick by not recruiting properly; failing to add experience to his coaching team; failing to replace outgoing fitness and set-piece coaches and failing to interrogate details of his new game-model, they are now failing him by having him continue in a job that is too much for him, destroying a season that remains salvageable. Meantime, the Glazers’ dividends continue unabated.

As such, the question isn’t so much who should be United’s next manager as can anyone manage United? Increasingly, it looks like the answer is – to quote Don Logan in Sexy Beast – “No”.

There are immediate on‑pitch issues: the team are unfit and their collective mentality weak; after years being lied to, the captain has finally broken; and the players hate the suits, the package toxic. Those are the minor problems.

Roughly, football management comprises five aspects: coaching, tactics, talent ID, pastoral care and presence. And, though no manager excels in every department, at this version of United surely they must. Moreover, given this is no job for a novice, track record is also important – and it might be here that Unai Emery’s candidature falls down, his brilliance in transforming mid‑ranking clubs undermined by failures at two high-profile basket cases.

Which brings us to pastoral care and presence. In the postwar era, only Erik ten Hag has won a trophy at United without tangible charisma, the size of the club now demanding it more than ever. This is not a quality associated with Eddie Howe, while Antonio Conte does not scream pastoral care and, brilliant though his career has been, he failed miserably at Spurs, the only environment he has inhabited that is remotely similar to United.

Should United miss Champions League qualification, financial strictures will make an elite coach and tactician essential. But in any event, football has changed such that the alchemic art of combining players and personalities no longer guarantees success.

Which leaves us with talent ID, player quality and intelligence being the best coaching and tactics there are. Of the 78 players purchased post-Ferguson, only Bruno Fernandes and perhaps Zlatan Ibrahimovic could be classed as cast-iron successes, so the next man – assuming United don’t target Emma Hayes, who embodies more of the right stuff than almost anyone else – must have a keen nose. Even Jürgen Klopp, the other manager who fulfils these criteria – and of all the damage Ed Woodward wreaked on United, turning him off was most costly – benefited from a department which delivered the greatest run of transfers ever.

Which leaves Mauricio Pochettino, who built something at Tottenham despite the environment, and Julian Nagelsmann. But had Pochettino not signed a new contract with the USA men’s team, the sense is that, as when Mourinho arrived at Old Trafford after years of speculation, he’s past his magical best, while Nagelsmann may be the free-thinking genius of his own imagination, just as he may be an emperor’s new clothes bluffer.

All of which is to say that failure has many fathers and, once ingrained, is not easily eradicated. Yet just as society has too much going for it to ever move quite beyond the pale, so too does Manchester United. Even the longest journey starts with one step; but does anyone have the nous to ensure it’s taken in the correct direction?

La Justicia Deportiva de Brasil sancionó este miércoles a Neymar, delantero del Santos, con dos partidos de suspensión por taparse la boca mientras discutía con un rival durante un encuentro del Campeonato Brasileño.

La segunda comisión disciplinaria del Tribunal Superior de la Justicia Deportiva sancionó a Neymar por "violar" la llamada 'Ley Vinicius', que castiga a los futbolistas que se tapan la boca para hablar en el campo y que se aplica en los torneos brasileños. La decisión aún puede ser recurrida al pleno del tribunal.

Neymar fue expulsado con roja directa antes de iniciarse la segunda mitad del partido del Brasileirao entre Santos y Bahía, disputado el sábado pasado en el estadio Vila Belmiro (1-1).

El ex jugador de Barcelona y PSG mantenía una discusión con el centrocampista del Bahía Rodrigo Nestor, con el que ya había tenido un encontronazo en la primera mitad, cuando en un momento determinado se tapó la boca para decirle algo.

Nestor se fue inmediatamente hacia el árbitro para contarle lo sucedido. El colegiado dijo que no vio nada, pero el VAR le avisó y Neymar fue expulsado.

Además, el acta del árbitro registró que, tras recibir la tarjeta roja, el atacante "corrió hacia el banquillo del equipo visitante para enfrentarse a su rival y tuvo que ser sujetado por sus compañeros".

El instructor del caso, Delmiro Campos, describió el comportamiento de la estrella del Santos como un "acto de ocultar lo que se dice, lo que vulnera la transparencia de la competición y la ética deportiva".

"Desde un punto de vista objetivo, el gesto de taparse la boca en un contexto de enfrentamiento dirigido al rival y la conciencia de la ilicitud son incuestionables", aseveró el instructor del caso.

Por su parte, la defensa de Neymar alegó que "no había animosidad ni un conflicto intenso entre los jugadores, que estaban intercambiando palabras en un contexto de bromas y buen humor".

"No todo gesto de taparse la boca está tipificado en la Ley Vini Jr., y el jugador se llevó la mano a la boca de forma instintiva", añadió el abogado Marcelo Mendes en el juicio. Algunos de los miembros de la comisión disciplinaria llegaron a plantear cuatro partidos de sanción, pero finalmente la mayoría le impuso dos.
