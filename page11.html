#470. Flick reactiva al Barça con Koundé todavía fuera

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

El FC Barcelona regresó a los entrenamientos después de dos días de descanso tras las derrotas ante el Paris Saint-Germain y el Real Madrid. La sesión en la Ciutat Esportiva Joan Gamper dejó varias novedades, con Jules Koundé todavía al margen, Pau Cubarsí trabajando con normalidad y la presencia de tres jóvenes del filial, ya habituales, en la dinámica del primer equipo.

La principal incógnita continúa siendo Koundé. El defensa francés siguió sin ejercitarse con el grupo mientras avanza en la recuperación del esguince de tobillo sufrida ante el Betis y que le impidió participar en los últimos encuentros en París y frente al Real Madrid. Su evolución durante los próximos días será determinante para conocer sus opciones de estar disponible ante el Deportivo Alavés este sábado en el Spotify Camp Nou. Aunque su presencia en el Clásico ya estuvo en duda hasta el último momento, el paso de los días le ofrece una nueva oportunidad para intentar llegar al compromiso ante el Deportivo Alavés. Su evolución marcará los plazos y determinará si está en condiciones de volver a competir.

La situación en la retaguardia preocupa al cuerpo técnico, que tiene actualmente a cuatro defensas afectados por diferentes problemas físicos: Espart, Eric Garcia, Koundé y Christensen. La última incorporación a la lista de bajas ha sido Xavi Espart, que estará aproximadamente siete semanas de baja después de sufrir una lesión en la sindesmosis del tobillo derecho durante el Clásico, tras una entrada de Tchouaméni.

La lesión de Espart supone un nuevo contratiempo para Flick, que necesita recuperar efectivos en una línea defensiva especialmente castigada. En este contexto, la posible vuelta de Koundé cobraría todavía más importancia, aunque el técnico deberá valorar su estado físico antes de decidir si puede contar con él ante el Alavés.

La nota positiva la protagonizó Pau Cubarsí. El central azulgrana, que decidió no viajar a la Gala del Balón de Oro en Londres el pasado lunes para recuperarse de un proceso febril, trabajó con normalidad con todo el grupo.

La sesión también contó con el centrocampista Ebrima Tunkara, el central Àlex Campos y el atacante egipcio Hamza Abdelkarim, tres jóvenes que completaron el grupo bajo las órdenes de Hansi Flick.

El conjunto azulgrana centra ahora sus esfuerzos en preparar el encuentro ante el Deportivo Alavés de este sábado (21.00 horas / Movistar +), con el objetivo de dejar atrás los últimos tropiezos y recuperar su mejor versión al frente del campeonato liguero. Tras el encuentro del sábado, la plantilla afrontará otros dos partidos de nivel ante el Aston Villa y el Atlético de Madrid antes del segundo parón liguero del curso.

#140. Carrick and Fernandes hold clear-the-air talks ahead of Chelsea fixture

Manchester United captain Bruno Fernandes was an unused substitute in their 3-0 defeat against Bournemouth after being dropped by Michael Carrick; Man Utd head coach will take charge of Saturday's game against Chelsea with Fernandes now in contention after talks on Tuesday

Manchester United head coach Michael Carrick and captain Bruno Fernandes have met face to face to clear the air, with both vowing to refocus on turning the club's season around.

Fernandes was an unused substitute after being dropped for the 3-0 defeat against Bournemouth on Sunday after he criticised Carrick's tactics following the 4-1 thrashing at Como in the Champions League.

Carrick had described his reasons for dropping his captain as "pure football".

"Picking the team is always a difficult decision," Carrick told Sky Sports. "We've got to make what we feel is the right one for the team to try to get the performance we want."

However, Carrick and Fernandes met at United's training ground on Tuesday, Sky Sports News has been told, for an open and frank discussion, with the head coach satisfied the animosity between them is over, although it is not known if the midfielder apologised for his public criticism.

Fernandes is now expected to be in contention for United's Premier League game against Chelsea at Stamford Bridge on Saturday.

The individual meeting between Carrick and Fernandes was in addition to an all-squad meeting, where there was a renewed determination from all the players and staff that performances have to improve immediately.

There was also a mutual admission, we are told, that there has been a squad-wide underperformance so far this season.

Carrick and the Man Utd players have been given the chance to turn their form around despite the under-pressure head coach thought to be on the brink after Sunday's defeat.

Sky Sports News' Danyal Khan at Carrington:

"Bruno Fernandes is absolutely back in contention. From what I understand and what I've been told from sources around Manchester United, Bruno Fernandes has been back to his best in the last 48 hours.

"Now, what does that mean, and how has that come around?

"It was quite out of character, you have to say, for him to come out last week, airing a lot of frustrations after the Como defeat.

"I would also assume that the reaction or lack of by the fans towards him on Sunday, when he's warming up, no shouts of Bruno from around Old Trafford, and then the boos, not just Fernandes, but also the players faced after Michael Carrick had gone down the tunnel.

"What I've been told, Bruno Fernandes has come back to Carrington since Monday. He was here on Monday to receive treatment to maintain his fitness, but since then all has seemingly been back to normal, and that is the message we have received at Manchester United, it is business as usual for everyone involved.

"I imagine he'll have a much bigger part than he did on Sunday against Bournemouth, and he is absolutely back in contention to play for Michael Carrick's side on Saturday."

Following the defeat to Bournemouth, Sky Sports' Gary Neville said Fernandes had let himself down.

"Eric Cantona was very unorthodox as a captain," he said. "Roy Keane could sometimes be explosive in his interviews after matches, but they weren't whingers about the tactics, about pressing, or excuse merchants.

"Bruno can't press. To come out and whinge after the game about the manager's tactics, when you've just lost 4-1, and a few thousand fans have paid thousands of pounds to travel over in that far corner and get wet through. I'm sorry, read the room, Bruno.

"This is not a time for whinging, throwing your arms up in the air. This is a time for you to stand up and say, that's not good enough. Be positive. Do something with a positive influence as a captain. But he's not.

"He's gone and basically thrown his manager under the bus the week after [Lisandro] Martinez did the same."

#476. Liga F y el fútbol paulista estrechan lazos con la vista puesta en el Mundial de Brasil 2027

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

El fútbol femenino español y brasileño siguen avanzando en su colaboración con un objetivo común: impulsar la profesionalización y favorecer el crecimiento de la disciplina a escala internacional. La Liga F y la Federación Paulista de Fútbol (FPF) han dado un nuevo paso en esta dirección con una semana de trabajo en Madrid y Barcelona, en la que una delegación brasileña ha podido conocer de cerca la organización de la competición española y las estrategias de varios de sus clubes.

La expedición, encabezada por el presidente de la FPF, Reinaldo Carneiro, junto a su vicepresidente, Mauro Silva, y la directora de Fútbol Femenino, Kin Saito, contó también con representantes de distintos clubes paulistas y de Petrobras, compañía vinculada al desarrollo de competiciones y proyectos de fútbol femenino en Brasil.

El encuentro da continuidad al acuerdo de colaboración firmado el pasado agosto en São Paulo, que busca fomentar el intercambio de conocimientos, compartir experiencias y generar nuevas oportunidades de desarrollo para ambas partes.

Conocer desde dentro el fútbol profesional español

Durante su estancia en la sede de Liga F, los representantes brasileños participaron en diferentes sesiones para profundizar en las claves del funcionamiento de la competición desde su conversión en profesional. La agenda abordó ámbitos como la estrategia comercial, la gobernanza, el marco jurídico, la organización de los partidos, el posicionamiento de marca y la relación con los aficionados.

La experiencia también incluyó una aproximación práctica a la gestión de un encuentro profesional. La delegación asistió al partido entre el Atlético de Madrid y el Madrid CFF para conocer de primera mano la coordinación y los procedimientos necesarios para organizar una jornada de Liga F.

El recorrido permitió analizar distintas formas de desarrollar el fútbol femenino desde la estructura de los clubes. En Barcelona, el FC Barcelona dedicó dos jornadas a compartir con la delegación su modelo de negocio, su estrategia comercial y su organización deportiva, además de mostrar el trabajo del primer equipo femenino en la Ciudad Deportiva Joan Gamper.

En Madrid, el Atlético abrió las puertas de sus instalaciones de Alcalá de Henares para explicar la evolución de su proyecto femenino, desde sus inicios hasta su actual dimensión deportiva y comercial. La visita permitió profundizar también en sus estrategias de marketing y posicionamiento y acercarse a la perspectiva de las propias futbolistas.

A estas experiencias se sumó una sesión a cargo de Sandro Arrufat, consejero del Costa Adeje Tenerife, centrada en las particularidades de un club femenino independiente. La comparación de estas tres realidades ofreció a los representantes paulistas una visión amplia de las diferentes estructuras que conviven en el fútbol profesional español.

La colaboración cobra especial relevancia ante la próxima Copa Mundial Femenina de la FIFA, que Brasil organizará del 24 de junio al 25 de julio de 2027. Será la primera vez que el país sudamericano acoja el torneo, con São Paulo entre las ocho sedes previstas.

La cita mundialista representa una oportunidad para reforzar las estructuras del fútbol femenino brasileño, ampliar su visibilidad y consolidar su crecimiento. En este contexto, el intercambio con Liga F pretende contribuir a la preparación del sector mediante la formación, la transferencia de conocimiento y la identificación de proyectos conjuntos.

Más allá de las visitas institucionales, ambas organizaciones aspiran a transformar el acuerdo en iniciativas concretas que impliquen tanto a las competiciones como a los clubes. Una apuesta a largo plazo que busca crear sinergias entre España y Brasil y avanzar hacia un fútbol femenino más profesional, sostenible y competitivo.

#478. Dimisión en Japón por 'culpa' de Infantino

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

El vicepresidente de la Federación Japonesa de Fútbol (JFA), Takeshi Okada, anunció este miércoles su dimisión, en medio de un desacuerdo con las recientes posturas adoptadas por la Confederación Asiática de Fútbol (AFC) respecto a la FIFA. Su salida se produce después de que la AFC expresara el viernes su apoyo a la candidatura de Gianni Infantino para un nuevo mandato al frente del organismo mundial.

Esta postura contrasta con la adoptada unas semanas antes por la AFC, que se había sumado a los organismos europeos y norteamericanos para criticar el proyecto de Infantino de abrir las competiciones más prestigiosas de la FIFA -incluida la Copa del Mundo- a inversores privados. Okada, exseleccionador de Japón en los Mundiales de 1998 y 2010, explicó que estas controversias son incompatibles con sus convicciones personales.

"A lo largo de mi carrera, he concedido gran importancia al valor social del deporte, convencido de que debía practicarse con integridad", declaró, antes de añadir que no puede aceptar esta brecha entre sus principios y las posturas adoptadas recientemente. El dirigente japonés precisó que su decisión ha sido largamente meditada y tomada por iniciativa propia, y no señala explícitamente el apoyo de la AFC como la causa directa de su dimisión.

#176. Un don de la oportunidad llamado Álvaro

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

El utrerano entró en la segunda parte contra el Recreativo y fue clave con dos asistencias

El Rayo Vallecano se tropezó en el Nuevo Colombino y fue Álvaro García quien lo levantó, por enésima vez. Beñat San José introdujo al extremo en el minuto 56 junto a Sergio Camello y Jorge De Frutos y fue clave para remontar el partido con dos asistencias, una a Alemao y otra al segoviano. Este jugador ya está acostumbrado a aparecer en momentos clave y el día de su 34 cumpleaños no fue una excepción.

Cumpliendo su novena temporada en la Avenida de la Albufera, Álvaro se ganó rápidamente el cariño de la afición gracias a su recorrido y desparpajo por la banda. Sus goles y asistencias han dado muchas alegrías al Rayo Vallecano y es que este jugador es de esos que aparece en el momento oportuno. Su primera puesta de héroe fue en la promoción de ascenso de la temporada 20/21, la última que vivió el Rayo Vallecano que hasta entonces se ha mantenido en Primera.

Álvaro fue el encargado de estrenar el marcador en la ida de las semifinales ante el Leganés, antes de los goles de Bebé. En la vuelta de la final, reconocida como una cita histórica por su adversidad, también abrió la lata, a pase de Emiliano Velázquez, en Montilivi. Antes del gol de Trejo que le dio el último ascenso al Rayo Vallecano.

En citas de máxima exigencia, como en los partidos ante Real Madrid o Barcelona, también ha aparecido. Álvaro ha anotado una diana y dado tres asistencias en los 13 encuentros que ha disputado contra los blancos, mientras que ha generado seis tantos (con reparto equitativo entre goles y pases de gol) contra el equipo dirigido en la actualidad por Hansi Flick.

Su capacidad de aparición adquirió una nueva dimensión en la Conference League. El utrerano anotó el primer gol de la vuelta a una competición europea ante el Neman Grodno en la fría Hungría. También anotó dos y dio una asistencia en la vuelta en Vallecas para asegurar la participación en la fase de liga. En total consiguió anotar seis dianas, igualando el récord histórico de Bolo que marcó seis goles en la Europa League con el primer Euro Rayo.

Álvaro García suma 327 encuentros con el Rayo Vallecano en los que ha anotado 51 goles y dado 52 asistencias. Las dos últimas contra el Recreativo de Huelva para darle el pase al cuadro franjirrojo a la siguiente ronda de la Copa del Rey. Con Míchel, Jémez, Iraola, Iñigo y ahora con Beñat, sigue apareciendo.

#75. Kylian Mbappé’s China crisis shows how sensitive Mao’s legacy remains | Rebecca Bailey

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Real Madrid forward was dropped by brands for comments Chinese media generally steered clear of outlining

It’s been a bad start to the week for the Kylian Mbappé. The France forward missed out on joining the elite roster of Ballon d’Or winners and instead became the latest high-profile addition to a far less illustrious list – that of foreign brands and individuals charged with “hurting the feelings of the Chinese people”.

The saga began when a video of the Real Madrid player making light of a meme involving him and China’s former leader Mao Zedong began spreading.

Within hours Mbappé found himself condemned online, before being unceremoniously dropped by brands, then held responsible for state television suspending the entire top Spanish league from its schedule. On Tuesday Mbappé apologised – “My comment was ill-judged, and I now understand that it hurt many people in China” – as did La Liga, which released a statement saying it “deeply regrets the offence that Kylian Mbappé’s recent comments have caused among Chinese fans”.

Foreign brands from Mercedes to Versace and celebrities from John Cena to Katy Perry have previously been sent to the sin bin for crossing China’s political lines on Tibet, Hong Kong and Taiwan.

Mbappé’s case shows how sensitive Mao’s legacy remains in China 50 years after his death – to the extent that most Chinese coverage of the affair does not even mention what the footballer said.

In the video, Mbappé references a meme of himself and “a guy … I think he was a Chinese dictator”, before a composite image of Mbappé’s face on Mao’s body pops up. (Mbappé has been nicknamed “the dictator” since his time at Paris Saint-Germain, a joke referring to his perceived influence at the club.)

The football app Dongqiudi was among the first to release a statement condemning the “inappropriate remarks”, but said: “Because his comments were offensive to China and the Chinese people, we cannot translate them.” Chinese media have reported on reactions such as the brand desertions, and the China Football Association’s lodging of a “stern protest” with Real Madrid – who apologised on Wednesday “to the Chinese people for any offence caused” – but have generally steered clear of outlining Mbappé’s precise offence.

The topic was still trending on Chinese social media on Wednesday morning, with one hashtag relating to the superstar’s apology boasting more than 600m views. Some posts asked variations on a simple question: “What did he actually say?”

Manya Koetse, editor of the Eye on Digital China newsletter, said in a post on X: “Many netizens without a VPN are still left in the dark about why all the talk about boycotting Mbappé is actually happening.”

Still, many expressed anger at what was characterised as disrespect for, as well as a misunderstanding of, China’s history. “For the majority of the [Chinese] people, Mao was and is still not seen as negatively as it is believed in the west,” said Mobo Gao, professor of Chinese studies at Adelaide University.

Mao was the founding leader of modern China, but his legacy includes radical policies such as the Great Leap Forward, an industrialisation policy blamed for a famine that killed as many as 30 million people by some estimates.

The ruling Communist party (CCP) maintains Mao was a revolutionary hero, though after his death it acknowledged another extreme policy, the violent Cultural Revolution, was a “grave mistake”.

Gao said: “There has been intense tension between Mao hatred and Mao support among the Chinese themselves. This tension is even more keenly felt since the economy has slowed.”

Some scholars have pointed to a resurgence of interest in Mao among younger Chinese, with a yearning for a time they regard as more idealistic and egalitarian – something the CCP is keen to keep in check.

Jennifer Pan, professor of communication at Stanford University, said the difficulty of doing independent social science surveys in the past decade meant “our understanding of what Chinese people actually think is weak”. Pan said: “Social media doesn’t fill that gap. It’s noisy, unrepresentative, and censored … What we see is that companies, platforms, broadcasters and the foreign ministry moved together quickly. Did this follow public anger, or did this produce public anger? Either is possible, and we can’t tell which came first.”

Whichever way round, the financial cost for Mbappé is the same. His face is a common sight on Chinese buses, in subways and in online ads, and his commercial deals could be worth “as much as $30m (£22.6m) per year”, said Mark Dreyer, author of Sporting Superpower, a book about China’s ambitions in the sector.

However, “as soon as someone else has decided you’ve crossed the line, it’s a race to who can cut ties quickest, who can be the most patriotic”, he said. “Then you have to kiss the ring – there really is no other way if you want to stay in the market.”

Mbappé’s and La Liga’s swift apologies suggest they are trying to avoid a fate like the NBA, whose lucrative pre-season matches were shut out of China for six years after the Houston Rockets’ then general manager Daryl Morey voiced support for Hong Kong pro-democracy protests in 2019.

La Liga may have better luck, with the state broadcaster CCTV saying it would listen to what Mbappé had to say, and “more importantly” watch what he does.

On Wednesday a foreign ministry spokesperson, Guo Jiakun, said: “We take seriously the apology the individual concerned has made.”

Dreyer said: “It doesn’t sound like it’s an instant ‘all is forgiven’. But it sounds like they’re going to be a little bit more, for want of a better word, reasonable.”

#158. Expectation vs reality: What if Man Utd have become a mid-table team?

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Michael Carrick remains under pressure at Man Utd after a poor start to the season; former midfielder is club's eighth permanent boss since Sir Alex Ferguson retired in 2013 but this week's Between the Lines explains why they have bigger problems

Michael Carrick has been given a stay of execution by Manchester United after the club considered making a fourth managerial change in four years following their defeat by Bournemouth.

Carrick, the club's eighth permanent boss since Sir Alex Ferguson's retirement in 2013, came under major scrutiny after their poor start to the season continued with the 3-0 loss at Old Trafford.

But what if United aren't an elite team underperforming - what if they are in fact a mid-table team whose results have made them look better than they are in the post-Ferguson era?

What if the managers aren't really to blame?

Please use Chrome browser for a more accessible video player

United's managers have paid the price for the club's struggles over the course of the last decade, but research for Between the Lines shows they have mostly overperformed in terms of points won in the Premier League relative to 'expected' points.

United's total of 669 points is the fifth-highest in the Premier League in that timeframe. But the Sky Sports expected points model, which factors a team's expected goals and expected goals against by game to determine the most likely outcome, puts United on 613, meaning they have taken 56 points more than expected.

In the Sky Sports expected table, a team is awarded three points when outscoring their opponent's xG by more than 0.5, one point if the margin is between -0.5 and 0.5 xG, and no points if their opponent outscore their xG by more than 0.5.

By way of example, although United beat Chelsea 1-0 at Stamford Bridge on April 18 last season, the xG was in Chelsea's favour by 1.57 to 0.29, meaning the 'expected' outcome was a United defeat.

Since 2016, only Everton have outperformed their expected points by a bigger margin than United in the Premier League.

This type of overperformance can be explained by a variety of factors, including clinical finishing, good goalkeeping, wasteful opponents and simply luck. But it has been a theme of the last decade at United and raises questions of their true level.

On a per-game basis, Carrick ranks as the biggest overperformer of their recent managers, averaging 1.85 points per game from an expected average of 1.37 over the course of his tenure.

He has underperformed this season, taking three points less than expected. But last season, he guided United to a third-placed finish on 71 points with an expected total of 61. United 'should' have finished two places lower, based on the expected table.

Erik ten Hag, Ralf Rangnick, Jose Mourinho and Ole Gunnar Solskjaer also overperformed relative to expected points while in charge of United, with Ruben Amorim the only exception since 2016.

Carrick's exploits last season raised expectations. If he could take United from seventh to third in the space of a few months, what could he achieve over the course of a full season?

But results flattered their performances - and not for the first time.

Last season was the eighth of the last 10 in which United have finished higher than expected, based on the quality of their performances. While United fans want a return to their glorious past, the underlying numbers betray a mid-table team.

Between 2021/22 and 2025/26, United's average expected finishing position was ninth. Our expected points model shows that in the 2023/24 season, when they finished eighth under Ten Hag, they should actually have finished significantly lower, in 14th.

United's consistently mediocre performance levels raise questions about the quality of their squad - and that despite having a net spend 40 per cent higher than any other club's since 2016 at £1.4bn.

United's net spend rank among Premier League clubs has been higher than their expected points rank in eight of the last 10 seasons. Damningly, they haven't registered a higher expected points rank than net spend rank in any of those seasons.

The numbers lay bare the club's poor recruitment over the last decade. The managers are not blameless, of course. But the issues at the club clearly run deeper than the men in the dugout.

"The reason people are there in the dressing room is because someone recommended them," said Sky Sports pundit Gary Neville on his podcast after Sunday's defeat to Bournemouth.

"Michael Carrick hasn't picked all those players out on that pitch. That's not Michael Carrick's job. So, who's the head of recruitment? Who's the sporting director?

"That's where all eyes should be looking now."

#154. Can clubs stop team news leaking out through FPL picks?

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Joao Pedro had been pictured in training before his shock absence from Chelsea's line-up last weekend

Chelsea head coach Xabi Alonso was caught off guard after Saturday's loss to Tottenham when asked whether he was aware that Joao Pedro's injury had been leaked through the popular Fantasy Premier League game.

His response was: "I am not into the fantasy team thing, so I didn't know that."

Accounts believed by FPL trackers to belong to Chelsea staff, a player and a relative of a player appeared to transfer out or bench Joao Pedro before the deadline.

One Fantasy Premier League manager, known online as FPL Karim, was among those who believed they had identified information by tracking selections made by a relative of Chelsea forward Morgan Rogers.

He said: "There were rumours that a player was missing from Chelsea's attack. I went to check Rogers' team, but he'd sold Joao Pedro two or three gameweeks earlier when he was injured. He never bought him back.

"Then I checked his brother's FPL team and saw that he'd sold Joao Pedro that gameweek and brought in Nicolas Jackson. I thought it was a bit weird. Why would you sell him when he's one of the most-owned forwards in the game?

"That's when I felt maybe it was Joao Pedro who was missing out. I posted about it, it went viral, and that was basically it."

Confirmation of the injury emerged after the FPL deadline had passed, meaning 1.7 million managers had already selected Joao Pedro as captain, which doubles a player's points return.

The absence also came as a surprise to many following Chelsea's 2-0 defeat by Tottenham, given Joao Pedro had been pictured in training during the week before the match.

Alonso later said the striker had missed the game because of "fluid on his thigh".

'I didn't know that' - Alonso on Joao Pedro's injury leak

Alonso is one of a number of managers who have become increasingly guarded when discussing team news in media conferences.

Last month, he said: "I prefer not to give more information than needed."

On that occasion, his side went on to lose 3-0 at Brentford, having kept several injury concerns, including one involving Joao Pedro, under wraps.

Yet such is the popularity of Fantasy Premier League - a game played by more than 11 million people according to official figures - that many staff members, players and relatives of footballers also take part.

By identifying leagues or teams linked to players, staff or family members through the game, which provides an open database of participants, it can be possible to infer what is happening behind closed doors at various training grounds.

Previously, the highest-profile case involved Jack Grealish's absence from Aston Villa's match against Leicester City in February 2021.

Leicester's manager at the time, Brendan Rodgers, admitted he had heard about the issue before the match, while Villa boss Dean Smith reportedly banned figures inside the club from playing FPL after the 2-1 defeat.

Later that year, Manchester City manager Pep Guardiola described the situation as "unethical and unprofessional".

There have been many further examples, claiming to suggest players from clubs like Arsenal, Liverpool, Leicester and Newcastle made revelatory changes to their teams.

Often that information is widely shared on social media, but it is also known that some top FPL players will withhold information for smaller circles to gain a competitive advantage in the game itself.

BBC Sport was able to find accounts that trackers claim are linked to staff, players and family members at clubs across the Premier League.

One of the experts in finding these club-linked accounts is FPL Focal, who told BBC Sport: "I am sure there are many more that have not been found. I have noticed some players have started using aliases. I've also had a couple of requests over the years to remove accounts, which I have always respected.

"The FPL leaderboard is completely public, so it is straightforward to look players up. Verifying that an account genuinely belongs to a player takes a bit more time."

Chelsea were subjected to three significant team news leaks during Liam Rosenior's short-lived tenure.

Two came before each leg of Chelsea's record 8-2 aggregate Champions League defeat by Paris St-Germain, while the third occurred before the Englishman's final game in charge.

Remarkably, it later emerged that former defender Marc Cucurella's barber had revealed both Cole Palmer and Joao Pedro were unavailable because of injury.

Since then, Chelsea have reminded players to be careful about who they share information with before matches.

The club have also strengthened security measures at the training ground, including building new fencing around first-team pitches.

Chelsea appeared to have got on top of their leak problems earlier this season, but the Joao Pedro episode serves as a reminder that information can still emerge in unexpected ways.

Clubs can remind players and staff to be careful about what they share and with whom, while some may seek to discourage employees from selecting players from their own club in Fantasy Premier League. The nuclear option would be to ban the game entirely.

There is no league-wide rule against people involved in the sport playing the game and the Premier League defers any issues that emerge as a club matter.

Yet any measures are likely to have limitations.

While clubs can influence staff behaviour, monitoring the activity of players' friends and family members is considerably more difficult.

Norwegian federation president distances self from Fifa presidency but believes governing body can be reformed

As the rain whips around Oslo’s government quarter, Lise Klaveness is shielding herself from the wind, trying to work out tactics. “Of course we cannot probably do everything alone, but we have a voice, we have the right to speak, we have the right to suggest, so let’s see,” she says. “We will at least try to be a spearhead.”

The president of the Norwegian Football Federation wants to re-engage Fifa, and other governing bodies, on the topic of remedy. A slightly arcane aspect of human rights policy in normal times, the importance of remedy became very clear in the aftermath of the World Cup in Qatar, when the families of those injured or killed in the work that led up to the tournament were left without financial compensation after Fifa chose to direct a legacy fund elsewhere.

At Klaveness’s prompting, Fifa conducted a review into the process. The report ultimately found the organisation did have a responsibility to provide remedy, but Fifa chose not to redirect the money. Klaveness wants to focus minds on the topic once again and make sure new practices are in place for Euro 2028, co-hosted by England, Scotland, Wales and the Republic of Ireland, as well as the 2034 World Cup in Saudi Arabia, where labour movement sources suggest there is little to no external scrutiny on worker’s conditions.

She is doing so at a delicate juncture. While Gianni Infantino has assured those listening that the “vast, vast majority” of Fifa’s electorate will back his re-election as president next year, many within the game regard this as an opportunity for change. Klaveness is one of them, having been a thorn in Infantino’s side since she first raised the topic of remedy on the floor of the Fifa congress in 2022 and having called for him to resign this summer after the scandals of the Balogun red card and the Fifa Forward Enterprise fiasco.

The 45-year-old Klaveness has been touted as a possible candidate in the Fifa presidential election, earning a perhaps less than desirable endorsement from Sepp Blatter. The disgraced former president said Klaveness “deserved the utmost respect” and that “the time has come for a woman to take over the leadership”. Klaveness responded by recalling that Blatter “wanted us to play with shorter shorts”. Her position on a run for president today is to almost, if not entirely, rule it out.

“My name has come up a few times and I take it as a sign that [people are concerned] about governance because we have been very outspoken about governance,” she says. “We’ve had a role internationally that is not the most efficient way to get votes. I think we’ve been very outspoken so this time around I don’t think that would be a possibility for me and it’s not been a real subject for me either.”

Not quite a no, then, but whether she runs or not Klaveness is a consistent champion for reform. She believes there are other candidates out there who could challenge Infantino and deliver change. She says it is crucial that any such individual is persuaded to stand before the deadline for nominations on 18 November. “It’s really important to have a candidate and one who can win,” she says. “We will welcome any candidate, but just putting up somebody who will lose is …” She shrugs. “I think it’s important to now discuss who can have a platform to win.

“There has been distrust, and not just in Europe, many other places than Europe,” she says of the challenge to Infantino. “Of course, it’s become very political, that’s why I refer to it as the Game of Thrones. Candidates need to have a base in different confederations. We don’t know how challenged Infantino can be until there’s a counter-candidate like that. But certainly there are people that can challenge him.”

On this day at least Klaveness is reluctant to make too many pugnacious remarks about Infantino or Fifa. “People ask me all the time about Infantino, but it’s not about him,” she says. “For me it’s about the governance going forward. He was elected on a platform of reform, which we really believed in, but it was not really implemented and that disappointed us.”

Klaveness says she wants any reforms around remedy to apply to all organisers and competitions. She talks about building alliances to back proposals that would ensure funds are set aside for remedy before a tournament takes place and that mechanisms are established for how any money should be distributed. She wants to submit such proposals at the next Fifa congress. “It should also be in Uefa and other places, but let’s start with Fifa, let’s start at the top, and now that governance is on everybody’s lips let’s make this part of the suggestions we will make to the congress, together or alone.”

A collaborative approach to achieving solutions, rather than deals struck behind closed doors, is Klaveness’s preferred way and on Tuesday the former midfielder, with 73 Norway caps, made her case on remedy to a gathering of politicians commemorating the 50th anniversary of the OECD’s guidelines for “responsible business conduct”. No longer the lone voice speaking out on the conference floor, Klaveness has allies and a growing number of people who – at least in private – would claim to share her values. That she also presides over two successful national sides doesn’t hurt.

Norway’s women’ face a playoff against Wales to qualify for next year’s World Cup. The men, meanwhile, won hearts and matches in the US this year. Klaveness recalls with some bemusement the World Cup experience. “I was on the road for seven weeks with 65 men,” she says. “I also saw Erling Haaland with a dead raccoon. Football is a lot of things, a sport, a competition, a cultural phenomenon. It’s also a social tool, but if we want the game to be for everyone there is going to be conflict.” It won’t be too long before that conflict resumes.
