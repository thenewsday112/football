#265. Fernandes gamble leaves Carrick to carry the can for United’s institutional failures | Will Unwin

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Leaving out his captain was doomed to fail but so are the club under the current leadership and the fans at Old Trafford know that all too well

Dropping your captain, who has held the team together for most of the past five years, is a bold move from a man whose job is on the line. It was very much a make-or-break decision from Michael Carrick to leave out Bruno Fernandes against Bournemouth. It did not work and Carrick knew the consequences if it failed and will probably pay the price for it, but he should not be alone.

Carrick said the decision to drop the Portuguese playmaker was purely based on football reasons but criticising his manager after the desperate defeat to Como cannot have helped. Clearly Carrick disagreed with his captain’s comments on the lack of pressing in the Champions League fixture. It was somewhat telling that Lisandro Martínez kept his place despite pointing out the director of football, Jason Wilcox, was a hindrance to the club the previous week.

Fernandes has been a long way from his best this season, with three goals and one assist in the Premier League, all in one game against Ipswich. It would have been justifiable to give someone else an opportunity in the No 10 role, especially when the squad contains far too many of those. But then, no one at United is in form, so keeping an angry Fernandes in position may have made more sense. United have won almost half the Premier League fixtures Fernandes has started since he joined in 2020, and less than a third when he is absent.

Some question Fernandes’ attitude based on his complaints when things are not going right, but that is his way of showing he cares. He may not be a leader in the Bryan Robson or Roy Keane mould but is certainly the one who sets the example, shown by a banner in the Stretford End depicting the two former captains and Eric Cantona alongside the word “STANDARDS”. Those have dipped here. This is a mid-table team, made worse by a lack of desire.

Everything was pedestrian in thought and execution. One promising attack lost momentum and United decided the most sensible course of action was to pass the ball all the way back to Senne Lammens, resulting in deserved boos. It felt like a moment that indicated Carrick’s days were coming to an end.

Hypotheticals are pointless, as was Youri Tielemans, Fernandes’ replacement. The central midfielder has not impressed since joining from Aston Villa in the summer and the extra responsibility for an experienced player did not spur him on. He was ponderous in midfield, repeatedly losing the ball as he struggled to cope with the pace of the game against a Bournemouth team eager to press and hunt in pairs. The No 10 is supposed to be a creative influence in the final third but the Belgian was a lost boy against men.

A poorly defended first goal resulted in the inevitable cutting by the cameras to a sullen Fernandes sunk into his seat on the bench. It was a goal that summed up the mood at Old Trafford, Rayan was left in space to volley home at the back post after Ayden Heaven needlessly gave away a free-kick. He looked miserable and the majority inside the ground felt exactly the same.

United lack pace across the pitch so require someone in key areas to speed up play, but Tielemans is not capable of doing so. His main influence on the match was getting booked in the first half, which all but confirmed his exit at the break. It was telling that Carrick turned to Matheus Cunha to take over the duties behind the labouring Bryan Mbeumo in the desperate hope he could spark his team into life.

Carrick has been left with an unbalanced squad, lacking quality in key areas. It would be hard to suggest on Sunday’s viewing that United were the Champions League team and Bournemouth in the Europa League. The Cherries can boast a wealth of smart recruitment, acquiring the likes of Adrien Truffert and Rayan – two players who would walk into the United team – for half a Harry Maguire.

The shambles of the third goal was on Lammens and Maguire, the former for running out too fast and the latter for not knowing how to run fast. Evanilson nipped in and flashed in the definitive game-ender. It sent Fernandes back to the bench with the expression of a man who had sipped sour milk. The final insult came when Carrick sent on the academy graduate Shea Lacey and left his captain to brood.

It is not solely one bottle; the whole dairy has curdled. Carrick can take some of the blame; the Glazers, Sir Jim Ratcliffe, Wilcox and Omar Berrada should receive more. United are not fit for purpose and the supporters’ ire was largely aimed at the directors’ box, demanding the owners sell up and go home, with slightly less savoury language. The Old Trafford faithful know where the fault lies, offering more creativity than any of their team with a fine repertoire of chants against the hierarchy, most pointedly at Wilcox, whose signings left Carrick in trouble before the season started.

Carrick had his name chanted, the full-time boos not for him. The manager – for now – did a lap of honour, cheered by the fans who jeered the players. Another era is nearing an end and finding reasons to be optimistic would be a greater waste of time than playing up front for United. Change will almost certainly come but it will not be enough.

#201. Ka Ying Lightning strikes to maintain unbeaten record and give David Hall a birthday treat

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Rising star continues perfect start to his career with narrow win up the straight under Andrea Atzeni

Ka Ying Lightning struck again at Sha Tin on Sunday to remain unbeaten and give David Hall the best birthday possible in the Class Three Lukfook Jewellery Night Banquet In Tang Palace Collection Handicap (1,000m).

Impressive in a pair of starts thus far, including a two-length romp on reappearance, the All Too Hard galloper gave another sizeable boost to his reputation with a gritty effort under Andrea Atzeni.

Quick away from the plum draw in stall 14, Ka Ying Lightning’s task was made tougher by a constant early challenge for the front by Jin Sheng, who pushed the pace for the first 600m.

It was of little consequence to the result, however, as after shaking off that rival approaching the 300m pole when still on the bridle, he gave generously to Atzeni’s urgings when challenged by newcomer Yee Cheong Bright, holding on by a neck.

It was an effort that can certainly be upgraded after a tough early challenge and Hall was very pleased with the performance on a timely day.

“We’ll take all the presents that come. If they’re going to be like that, it’ll be a big birthday, won’t it?” Hall said after his improver took his record to three from three.

“He did a pretty good job today. I think he got taken on a little bit, which wasn’t ideal. It would have been better for him to have a trail and then he got challenged by a horse that had been trialling really well. So, he knuckled down and fought strongly and he’s still unbeaten.”

Hall is now focusing on a new challenge for his rising star’s next run after racing exclusively over the straight 1,000m, with plenty of excitement surrounding his immediate future from a stable in rocking form.

“First time in Class Three, I kept him to what he was used to and I think it’s time for him to go around the corner now. His next run will probably be 1,200m – when that will be, I’ll just see how the horse is,” Hall said.

“The 1,200m will be interesting now. As I said, he’s got to go around the bend, he’s got to go a little bit further, but I’m quietly confident that 1,200m and 1,400m will be more his game. So, looking forward to it.”

Meanwhile, Pierre Ng Pang-chi maintained his one-win championship lead over Caspar Fownes with a double, headlined by Storm Mirror collecting back-to-back wins in the Class Lukfook Jewellery Heirloom Fortune Collection Handicap (1,400m).

The removal of the blinkers has been the making of the Deep Field galloper, who built on a modest reappearance with an all-the-way success on his second-up run two weeks ago.

With apprentice Nichola Yuen Hang-yiu’s 7lb claim, the four-year-old reappeared off 2lb lower in the weights and it turned out to be an inspired decision by Ng, as his improver never looked likely to be caught.

Smashing out of barrier seven, the pair were soon in control and turned into the straight with a commanding advantage. There was a brief moment of worry when Storm Mirror shifted out abruptly near the 150m, but Yuen was quick to correct him to a cosy two and three-quarter lengths.

“We were just hoping he would jump a bit sharper than last time, which he did today, so he got a very easy and comfortable lead,” Ng said. “I just told Nichola to let him roll along, breathe properly around the bend and in the straight, just roll him out a bit.

“He was a little bit shy around the 200m mark – he shied away from something – but Nichola managed to change her left hand whip and brought him back in. He improved from last run and hopefully he can slowly improve more in the future.

“We just hoped to add some more rating points today so we’d have more options for next run.”

The win was a part of a double for Ng, who had watched Smart Fat Cat shed his maiden at the 16th attempt in the Class Four Lukfook Jewellery Love Is Beauty Collection Handicap (1,600m) earlier on the card.

Having just his second run for the stable, the Starspangledbanner galloper was given a pearler of a ride by Karis Teetan, who made the smart decision to roust his mount along from midfield around the bend to get into a challenging position.

Charging down the outside under hard riding, Smart Fat Cat struck the lead 350m out and kept up the gallop to fend off the late challenge of Kingman Reef by half a length.

With that under his belt, Ng is hoping the best is yet to come from the still raw five-year-old.

“Once he got to the front he was a bit shy, but luckily there was another horse coming in a little bit to give him some pressure so he stayed on,” Ng said. “I think he’s got a little bit more to learn – he learned to be a bit more relaxed today, but I’m hoping he learns to really drop [the bridle] in the future.”

Storm Mirror’s earlier win was also part of a double for Yuen, who rounded off the card by riding Ricky Yiu’s Packing Glory to success in the Class Three Lukfook Jewellery Beloved Collection Handicap (1,400m).

#205. Brenton Avdulla boosts hopes of licence extension with Group Two win on Raging Blizzard

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Australian jockey secures much-needed feature success on John Size’s sprinter in Sunday’s Premier Bowl at Sha Tin

Brenton Avdulla boosted his hopes of securing a licence extension with a deserved feature success aboard consistent sprinter Raging Blizzard in Sunday’s Group Two Lukfook Jewellery Premier Bowl (1,200m) at Sha Tin.

Riding on a part-season licence after sliding down the championship standings with 21 wins last term, Avdulla lifted the John Size-trained Raging Blizzard to a narrow victory over Ka Ying Attack under top weight of 135lb.

The Australian jockey, whose Hong Kong licence expires in February, lauded Raging Blizzard after the seven-year-old’s career-best triumph.

“There are two horses that, every season, they keep turning up for me. They don’t win very often but him and Beauty Joy, they just turn up every season and run against the best and I said to connections that ‘I think it’s his turn today’,” Avdulla said.

“For a jockey in Hong Kong, you’ve got Zac [Purton] and Hughie [Bowman]. They get the best rides and the best horses in those big races but if you can have a ride in the good races, all the way through with a Blizzard or a Beauty Joy, they just help you all the time. You’re always in the prize money and I’m never far away with him.

“To actually win a race and a Group Two, it’s very special.”

Settled near the rear in the field of nine, Raging Blizzard made ground one-off the rail early in the straight before Avdulla hooked him around the heels of Copartner Prance to launch his run down the centre of the track.

Pacesetter and $2.8 favourite Ka Ying Attack booted clear, establishing a lead of three lengths approaching the 150m, but the $4.6 second favourite Raging Blizzard loomed as a big threat.

He wore down the David Hall-trained Ka Ying Attack to hit the front late and score by a head, with Crimson Flash running on late for third, beaten half a length.

“I just thought it was the easiest race he’s been and Hally’s horses have been in good form, but today, carrying the 135lb, second up and 1,200m – I thought today was D-Day for him and we got the right run through and he was able to get the job done,” Avdulla said.

Raging Blizzard handed Size a record-extending fifth Premier Bowl success after the master trainer’s previous wins with the Avdulla-ridden Sight Success (2023), Hot King Prawn (2018), Mr Stunning (2017) and Rich Unicorn (2011).

Since his previous win in January last year, Raging Blizzard had been placed seven times – including three behind the world’s best racehorse, Ka Ying Rising.

“It’s a bit of justice for him – he’s tried so hard in all his races. He’s been unlucky running into some very nice horses constantly, but eventually, he’s rewarded for his consistency. So, It was quite satisfying to see him win this race,” Size said.

“It was probably a special event for a lot of the connections, so we’re quite happy and relieved for the horse to win a race and for the owners, who have been believing in him for such a long time.

“He’s got a lovely temperament, he’s in beautiful condition most of the time and he’s sound and he does very well. He’s a pleasure to work with. But just race day, he just lacks the extra couple of rating points to mix it with the big ones.”

More clashes with Ka Ying Rising loom when Raging Blizzard progresses to the Group Two Jockey Club Sprint (1,200m) next month and December’s Group One Hong Kong Sprint (1,200m).

“He’s got no options, so he’ll go there and it’s a stepping stone to the second Sunday in December,” Size said.

Avdulla produced a top ride to bag the first leg of his double, guiding Australian import Pocketing to his first Hong Kong win in the Class Three Lukfook Jewellery Miss Hong Kong Love Yourself Collection Handicap (2,000m).

The David Hayes-trained gelding stormed home from midfield on the fence approaching the turn to beat Big Return by a neck.

“The last run was very good. He ran home strongly for fourth and then today he got a great ride from Brenton and the 2,000m, good barrier, good ride – he won. I’m hoping now over distance and fit, he can do it again,” Hayes said.

“[Avdulla] was very happy when the mid-race pressure came on that allowed the race to suit the horses from midfield and back – they went far too fast down the back straight.”

A Group Two winner pre-import, Pocketing was Hayes’ first Hong Kong winner for Yulong Investments owner Zhang Yuesheng.

“I’ve trained winners in Australia for him. He’s a lovely man and a huge owner,” Hayes said.

“I’ve got another horse for him that will be racing in one month’s time, so to have a couple of nice horses for the big owner, is a thrill.”

Elsewhere on Sunday’s card, injury-plagued galloper Megastar Heart backed up his stunning first-up win with a dominant success in class record time.

The Australian import made it back-to-back Class Three victories by two lengths under Luke Ferraris, boosting trainer Caspar Fownes’ confidence in him reaching a rating of 100 in the future.

“Like I said the last time he won, he’s been one of the toughest horses I’ve had to train through my career, so for us we get quite emotional and it’s fulfilling to get these types of horses to perform,” Fownes said.

“I’m confident he’s going to get to triple figures but he could be even better than that going forward, if he stays sound. He’s won it pretty soft, he’s run good time and he’s just going to keep improving.

“His next race will be on the 14th of November in the Panasonic Cup. If things stay the way they are, he’s going to be a live chance in Class Two which is awesome.”

Megastar Heart capped a double for Fownes after he struck earlier with Tycoon Express. He sits second in the championship standings on 13 wins – one behind leader Pierre Ng Pang-chi.

Trainer Ricky Yiu Poon-fai also recorded a double at Sha Tin courtesy of Chill Easy and Packing Glory. Chill Easy was the second leg of a brace for jockey Jerry Chau Chun-lok, who won earlier on Danny Shum Chap-shing’s debutant Happy Pegasus.

#298. Ian Holloway’s circus: why the manager’s antics are no laughing matter for fans

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Swindon manager has a history of viral outbursts but fans want a united club and manager they can support

In Ian Holloway’s often madcap managerial orbit, this was simply the latest in a steady succession of eccentric outbursts. Eyes bulging as he spoke, he boomed the letter “D” and promptly stabbed himself in the chest with an imaginary dagger. Next came the letter “M” alongside a blow from another invisible knife. Finally, a “W” followed by a jab to the heart. “Dead. Man. Walking,” he declared, pausing for effect between words and briefly falling into an Oscar-worthy zombie stupor. “That’s how it feels.”

To an offbeat press conference genre containing seagulls following trawlers and self-proclaimed special ones, it was a crazy new entry worthy of its subsequent viral nature. Holloway at his finest.

The clip was widely shared, there is every chance you saw it this month. The general perception, from those of a neutral stance, was that one of football’s great characters had lost the plot. To those in the room, there was a sense that it was merely the exuberant mannerisms of a quirky manager during a tricky period. But for a large section of Swindon fans, it prompted an unamused eye roll. Here we go again. The circus tent is pitched firmly in town.

Supporting a Holloway-led club is an interesting assignment. Within weeks of taking the Swindon job two years ago on Sunday, he began constructing a hostile wall towards fans by reminding them that they could “go bowling” if they did not like his methods. Last December, he had to be restrained by his players during an altercation with a fan, while he has made a habit of telling them to “jog on” if overly vocal in their criticism.

When his side picked up a much-needed point against Walsall last week, the goalkeeping coach held him back from another confrontation with supporters. Asked about it, he goaded reporters into putting his address online so that disgruntled fans could have it out with him: “I don’t really care anymore.” So, when season‑ticket holders found their phones pinging with amused messages containing the same viral Holloway zombie clip, their lack of laughter could be forgiven.

“When you get these soundbites from Ian Holloway, outside the Swindon Town world it’s all about what a great character he is, how he says it like it is, and how it must be great to have him as manager,” says Rich Pullen, founder and co-host of the largest Swindon podcast, Loathed Strangers. “But, to a growing percentage of the Swindon community, it’s exhausting and it takes away from any of the good.

“If Swindon win, post-match he often uses it as an opportunity to have a go at the fans. It’s got to the stage where you can’t even enjoy a win without Ian Holloway being unable to hold himself from criticising fans. The easy thing to do is ignore him, but it’s almost impossible because he is everywhere. He goes viral and everyone always thinks: ‘Oh, isn’t that funny.’ Well, no. People are tired of the excess noise.”

This time last week, there was a thought that the M4 derby homecoming trip to Bristol Rovers on Saturday might hasten a possible end to Holloway’s Swindon tenure. Winless in eight games in all competitions, the discontent was not expected to abate on the preceding midweek trip to high-flying Barnet. The 3-1 victory that followed – Barnet’s first league defeat of the season – was a huge shock, thanks in no small part to the heroics of the goalkeeper, Connor Ripley, who recently declared the whole squad would “run through a brick wall” for Holloway.

Swindon might have followed it with another win, at Rovers’ Memorial Stadium, only for Matty Virtue to miss an open goal 90 seconds into the match. The forgettable goalless draw that played out at least extended Swindon’s unbeaten run to three games and kept them safely mid-table in League Two. Holloway was right to savour the improved performances, but the malaise runs deeper.

The manager had said something far more significant immediately after his viral soundbite when he continued: “The owner’s a dead man walking as well and that over‑complicates everything here.”

Even those seeking a more composed figure in Holloway’s place agree the manager is just one cog in a machine many argue has been misfiring for some time. Days after the zombie rant, the Swindon Town Supporters Trust declared the club had “lost our soul, our hope, our passion”, and urged Holloway’s comments about the Australian owner, Clem Morfuni, to “be a wake-up call”.

Since saving the club from probable administration in 2021, Morfuni has become a target of increasing fan indignation, failing to sell up and adding millions to the club’s debt. “Whilst the short-term focus may be on Ian Holloway, the bigger picture is above the role of the manager,” concluded the Trust, pointing to “a wedge with supporters so obvious that even the manager openly talks about it”.

In such a context, the uptick in form is merely one – albeit very welcome – aspect among wider misgivings. Because what fans really want is a united club and manager they can get behind; not to go jogging on.

#326. Spurs boss Ho wants 'more support and training' for WSL referees

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

'Sometimes you need luck' - Should Arsenal's penalty have been given?

Tottenham manager Martin Ho says Women's Super League referees "need more support" as he vented his frustration over a controversial penalty decision in their derby defeat by Arsenal.

A 2-1 win for the Gunners at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium on Saturday was settled by Alessia Russo's spot-kick in the 56th minute.

Referee Phoebe Cross awarded the decision after Lisa Baum's shot bounced off the post and Cathinka Tandberg's clearance hit Spurs team-mate Ella Morris' outstretched hand.

According to the laws, if the ball hits a hand after it was played by a team-mate, a penalty should not be awarded.

Ho said: "My interpretation of the rule is if your team-mate hits it and your team-mate is one yard away, time and distance is taken into account and it's not a penalty.

"That's what we were told at the start of the season in the referee's meeting. I asked the question, 'how is it [a penalty] from that distance?' They couldn't give me an answer on the pitch.

"I said I would come and speak to them after the game because I want to understand it, I want to understand what they're seeing. Then I can accept a decision that is there. When that decides a game, it's frustrating, because it's not within the laws of the game."

Ho admitted WSL referees have "a hard enough job" to do, but believes the standards have "got worse than last year".

There is currently no use of video assistant referees (VAR) or goalline technology in the WSL, while not all officials are full-time.

Earlier this month, the Guardian reported, external the wait for full-time WSL referees was set to continue until 2029 despite a recent funding boost.

Asked how standards could improve, Ho added: "They need more support and more full-time [referees].

"I understand there is probably a rule where they can't do more than one game on a weekend and that's nothing against any of the referees, but they had an elite referee [Lisa Benn] on the side the pitch. For a big game, put them in the middle.

"I think that's where they need more support, more training, because when you look at most decisions that are going on, there's a lot of controversy in a lot of games.

"We got two bookings in the first half but we were being dragged back by the shirt by Arsenal players and didn't get anything. That's probably the frustrating part."

After recording four wins in a strong second campaign in Hong Kong, Crimson Flash was sent off the $5.6 third favourite in his return but found the 1,000m too sharp when fifth to Colourful King in the Group Three National Day Cup.

Atzeni said the Mark Newnham-trained gelding also didn’t appreciate being crowded in the middle stages and having to be steadied to avoid another runner.

The Sardinian jockey has high hopes for Crimson Flash improving on Sunday in what he rates a wide-open contest.
