#272. Ka Ying Attack and Ka Ying Lightning spearhead David Hall super-squad for Sha Tin

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The Australian trainer has the best strike rate in the city and unleashes two of his smartest prospects on Sunday’s card

David Hall has enjoyed a fine start to the season and unleashes improving sprinter Ka Ying Attack and excitement machine Ka Ying Lightning in a bid to keep the good times rolling at Sha Tin on Sunday.

The Australian trainer sits seventh in the trainers’ premiership on eight winners for the season thus far, with his strike-rate of 38.5 per cent being the strongest in the city.

Hall is well aware things can change very quickly in racing, but his stable is bouncing at present and he is enjoying the start for what it is.

“Every day is a different day racing wise – we had a couple of hard-luck stories on Monday, so perhaps it could be better. We’ve had a good start and everyone is a bit positive around the place, so yeah it’s quite good,” Hall said.

Hall’s team is undoubtedly spearheaded by the smart Ka Ying Attack, who bids to continue his rapid progression and to snag a fourth win on the bounce in the feature Group Two Lukfook Jewellery Premier Bowl (1,200m).

The Too Darn Hot galloper ended last season with a pair of impressive victories, starting the sequence with a two-and-a-quarter-length romp over track and distance, before stepping up to 1,400m for a similarly cosy success in July.

He was drawn wide on return two weeks ago and was forced to take his medicine and sit towards the back of the field, contrary to his usual tactics. That decision proved a stroke of genius, however, as he showed a remarkable turn of foot in the closing stages to scuttle up the rail and power clear after being short of room for much of the straight.

Ka Ying Attack has really found his groove, and with his confidence sky high at present, Hall is keen to keep pressing and see where this sequence can take him – especially with the notable absence of Ka Ying Rising on Sunday.

“He was in the zone towards the end of last year and he’s come back and showed that he’s gone right on with that form,” Hall said.

“When they get to that sort of rating, there’s not a huge amount of options. Of course, a Group Two sprint without Ka Ying Rising around, it’s a bit shallow and it’s a bit of an open opportunity at handicap conditions.

“The horse is fit and well, and he’ll take his chance. He’s more confident now, he was always a bit nervous around other horses. He had the blinkers on, visor on. He was a little bit immature, but he’s got stronger and stronger, not only physically, but mentally too. Now that he knows how to be a racehorse, he can show himself off a hell of a lot better. That’s taken him a long way.”

Any top sprints beyond Sunday’s Premier Bowl is likely to see Ka Ying Attack bump into the world’s best sprinter Ka Ying Rising and Sunday will be pivotal in deciding where Hall’s progressive galloper is likely to go next.

Options like the Group One Hong Kong Sprint (1,200m) at the Hong Kong International Races (HKIR) could come into view by winning on Sunday and with his galloper a much stronger, more mature and confident model, Hall thinks the best is yet to come.

“You’ve seen with the horse, he’s quite flexible with what you can do with him now. He went up the inside of horses last start and has become a real racehorse now. He’s got plenty of strings to his bow and while he’s got a light weight in a race like this, you never know,” Hall said.

“The outcome of this race will plot his path, of course, but when they’re in such good form you just don’t know where they’re going to get too. The race has probably come round at the right time, so we’ll see what it brings us.”

Hall will also unleash exciting youngster Ka Ying Lightning, who defends his unbeaten record in the Class Three Lukfook Jewellery Night Banquet In Tang Palace Collection Handicap (1,000m).

The son of All Too Hard made a big impression on debut when charging home from miles back to win by half a length, and he bettered that performance by a significant margin when lowering the colours of Jedi Spurs on reappearance.

He pulled two clear of Jedi Spurs, who also holds a big reputation, and although stepping up in distance is certainly in his future, Hall is in no rush to change a winning formula when he breaks from the plum draw in stall 14 on Sunday.

“It gets tougher each time, up in the ratings and up in class. He’s handled the straight very well – I’ve tossed it around whether we increase his distance, which will happen at some point, but at the moment he’s handling the straight so well, we thought we’d give him another spin down the 1,000m,” Hall said.

Power Of Vitam, Great Legend, Private Wealth, Flying Amani and Incanto Star round off a busy day for Hall.

The Flamingos have recovered from their opening defeat to Poland with a victory over Puerto Rico, but Friday's encounter represents a significantly tougher test against a side that has won the competition four times and is chasing a fifth crown.

Both countries are making their ninth appearances at the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup, having participated in the inaugural edition in 2008. However, North Korea's history in the competition has been particularly impressive, with the Asian side establishing themselves as one of its most successful nations.

Nigeria, meanwhile, will be hoping to build on their best-ever finish of third place in 2022 and keep their ambitions of making another deep run in the tournament alive.

Oluwakemi Adegbuyi opened the scoring in the 25th minute, rewarding Nigeria's attacking efforts, before substitute Precious Oscar sealed the win in stoppage time.

The result lifted the Flamingos to three points after two matches and restored belief within the camp ahead of their decisive meeting with North Korea.

North Korea's ability to score through different players also means Nigeria cannot afford to concentrate exclusively on one attacking threat. Busari's team will need to maintain their defensive shape, communicate effectively and avoid the individual mistakes that could allow the champions to take control of the contest.

North Korea lead the group with six points and a goal difference of +11, followed by Poland in second place with three points and a goal difference of -4.

Nigeria occupy third position, also with three points even with their goal difference of +1.

Poland currently occupies second place. Even though Nigeria and Poland are tied with 3 points each, the tournament regulations use the head-to-head result as the primary tiebreaker when teams are level on points.

It gives them a stronger overall margin than Poland. Puerto Rico sit bottom without a point, having conceded eight more goals than they have scored.

With the final group fixtures set to determine the teams advancing to the knockout stage, Nigeria's meeting with North Korea and Poland's clash with Puerto Rico will have a direct bearing on the final standings.

What Nigeria need to qualify for the knockout stage

The Flamingos remain firmly in contention for a place in the next round, but the result against North Korea will be crucial to their chances of progressing automatically.

A victory would guarantee Nigeria qualification for the knockout stage. However, if Poland also defeat Puerto Rico, all three teams could finish the group with six points, making the results between the three sides and the applicable head-to-head tie-breakers decisive in determining their final positions.

In that scenario, Nigeria's margin of victory over North Korea could become particularly important, as the goal difference within the three-team comparison may determine which side finishes ahead of the others.

A draw would take Nigeria to four points and leave them dependent on the result of Poland's match against Puerto Rico. If Poland lose, the Flamingos would finish second behind North Korea. However, a Polish draw or victory would leave Nigeria third, with their chances of advancing then dependent on the tournament's best-third-placed-team ranking.

A defeat would leave Nigeria on three points, meaning their hopes of progressing could depend on the outcome of the other group matches and their position among the third-placed teams across the tournament.

Consequently, avoiding a heavy loss is important, particularly given the possibility that overall goal difference could become a factor in determining qualification.

The Flamingos must therefore approach the fixture with a balance of ambition and caution. While a win would put their fate beyond doubt, they must avoid allowing North Korea's attacking strength to turn the match into a one-sided contest.

Head-to-head: Nigeria and North Korea have one win each

Despite North Korea's impressive record at the tournament, previous meetings between the two nations at the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup suggest that Nigeria have the capacity to compete against the Asian side.

The teams have met twice at the competition, with each nation recording one victory.

Their first encounter came on 5 September, 2010, when Nigeria claimed a thrilling 3-2 win.

Ngozi Okobi scored in the third and 79th minutes, while Francisca Ordega added Nigeria's second goal in the 77th minute. North Korea responded through Kim Su-gyong in the 28th minute and Kim Kum-jong in the 58th, but the Flamingos held on to secure the victory.

North Korea gained revenge six years later when the sides met on 8 October, 2016. Ri Hae-yon scored a hat-trick, finding the net in the 30th, 45th and 83rd minutes to inspire a comprehensive 3-0 victory for the Asian side.

The historical record is therefore evenly balanced, with Nigeria winning once and North Korea claiming the other victory. There have been no draws between the teams at the tournament.

Although those encounters involved different generations of players and offer no guarantee of what will happen on Friday, they provide a reminder that Nigeria have previously overcome North Korea on the World Cup stage, and should be a source of motivation for the current crop of players.

#325. Cathinka Tandberg: ‘Spurs fans hate Arsenal. That’s how it is’

📅 Oct 22, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Norwegian forward might look to Harry Kane for inspiration on the pitch, but when north London derby comes around, she needs no lessons on what the rivalry means

“It means more than just three points. It’s for the whole club. It’s for the whole place. It’s for the whole of Tottenham.” One only needs to be in the presence of Tottenham Cathinka Tandberg for a few minutes to feel the passion that emanates from her when it comes to this weekend’s north London derby. As a lifelong Spurs fan, the forward understands more than most the intensity of the rivalry that exists between her side and Arsenal, one that she cannot wait to experience once again on Saturday.

“It will just be an incredible feeling,” she tells Moving the Goalposts. “I think you will feel a few nerves that you maybe don’t feel in a normal game but that’s good.

“All the Spurs fans hate Arsenal and Arsenal hate Spurs. That’s how it is. You want to show that you’re the best team – simple as that. You just want to fight for the club, the badge and for the people around you.”

It has been a significant 13 months for the 22-year-old who made her dream move to London in September 2025. After catching the eye as a goalscorer in the Damallsvenskan at first Linköping and then Hammarby, the Norwegian was signed by Martin Ho on deadline day to bolster the team’s attacking options. She made her mark on her first start, scoring a spectacular 40-yard goal at Everton.

“I settled really well,” she reflects. “I’m really enjoying it and want to push myself forward. It’s been a tough start [to this season] and I haven’t really got what I wanted. But there’s loads of games left. I’m still young. I’m still improving and learning. I just have to trust the process and trust what I’m surrounded by.

“It’s a big difference from Sweden,” she continues. “You meet World Cup players week in, week out, so you have to perform every week if you want results. I feel like my physicality has changed a lot. I’ve got stronger and fitter. Playing under Martin has made me think more away from the goals. I think more about how I play with the ball, how I press and all of those things that I maybe didn’t focus on before … it’s a bit like Harry Kane. The older he gets, he has developed his game as well. He needed to be deeper in the pitch because that’s how they wanted to play and then he has to be better with the ball. That’s a bit how I also need to be. I can’t only just stand in the box. I need to be good everywhere else.”

Spurs have been transformed under Ho and finished fifth last season. However, with success comes expectation, ambitions that Tandberg clearly shares but with a sense of perspective.

“Tottenham has always been a Champions League club for me,” she says. “But I’m a fan so I can’t really talk for the other players. When I came to the club, I said that I want to get top three. But you also need to be realistic and to see where the team has been before. The transformation that Martin has done in a season is unbelievable. I don’t think anyone else could do that. That tells you everything about him. He’s one of the greatest coaches I’ve worked with and I think everyone that comes here, one of the reasons is because of him. We all trust him and the process.”

That faith is currently being tested. After an unbeaten start, Tottenham have suffered back-to-back defeats, conceding six goals to both London City Lionesses and Chelsea.

“You can call it a dip in form but I still feel like we also showed our qualities in those games,” Tandberg says. “It’s just about ourselves. We maybe need to change our mentality a bit, find that fire and dig a bit deeper when we play those top teams. I feel like maybe it’s good that it’s come because it had to at some point. It’s about how we respond.

#351. Who is close to a yellow card suspension in Champions League?

📅 Oct 22, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Arsenal forward Kai Havertz is among the players to have received two yellow cards in this season's Champions League

Players will receive a one-match ban should they receive four yellow cards before the semi-finals of the Champions League.

This rule runs from the start of the league phase until the end of the quarter-finals. After then, all yellow cards are wiped out and players cannot be suspended for multiple yellow cards.

The same rules apply for both the Europa League and the Conference League.

During the opening three rounds of Champions League, 16 players received two yellow cards, meaning they are now two cautions away from a one-match ban.

Shakhtar Donetsk midfielder Marlon Gomes is on three yellow cards, putting him on the brink of a ban.

Players can also be suspended if they pick up six, eight or 10 yellow cards. This would also result in a one-match suspension every time a player hits an even number of yellow cards above four.

Red cards result in a one-match suspension, with Bayern Munich's Michael Olise missing his side's clash with Arsenal after being sent off against Viking in the previous round of fixtures.

Uefa may increase the length of a red card ban for serious offences such as violent conduct.

#302. Shola Ameobi: ‘I thought: how can I be most useful to Newcastle? Then the cog started turning’

📅 Oct 22, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

From experiencing racism growing up in Newcastle to becoming a derby hero and now a member of the executive team, the former striker has been on a remarkable journey

“There were times where I feared for my life as a kid, being harassed and chased by different groups of people,” Shola Ameobi says. “There were situations where I had to sprint to get away from groups of lads who didn’t like me because of what I looked like.”

Ameobi is recalling growing up in Newcastle during the late 1980s and 1990s, having moved from Nigeria aged six. There were lots of moments like that.

“It was almost as if I had to hide myself to be able to do what I love to do,” he says. “It wasn’t a very nice feeling, having to worry about getting from A to B. It was just part of my world. I had to be more aware of my surroundings and what I was doing.”

Ameobi’s “A” was often the family home, situated within a few pitch lengths of Newcastle’s St James’ Park, and “B” was frequently the club’s base at the time in Benwell, where he would head for academy training.

“It built incredible resilience in me,” he says. “That really helped me in a sporting context in my career, but also as a person, understanding that, irrespective of what people thought of me, what really mattered was the value I put on myself.”

That young boy running in fear became the scorer of 79 goals in 397 appearances for Newcastle including seven against their local rivals, Sunderland. The likelihood is that some of those he fled from while wearing a club tracksuit subsequently cheered him.

Since July, Ameobi has been Newcastle’s head of corporate affairs, after seven years as the loans coordinator. His role is to ensure “we’re doing things that are valuable not only to us as a football club but to the region”.

He adds: “We want to make this club the heartbeat of this city. But by being the heartbeat of the city, it’s making sure we integrate well into everything the city is about, whether that’s civically, whether that’s through our sport or through our community engagement.”

Ameobi’s job switch followed the 100-day review conducted by the CEO, David Hopkinson. At the end of a preliminary discussion, Hopkinson told Ameobi to take the weekend to consider things. But Ameobi, while grateful for the “level of trust and commitment”, pushed back, wanting to consider what the role would look like in practice. Six months and plentiful discussions later, he accepted.

For Ameobi, the leap to an executive position was not as huge as it might appear. He started his previous role in the Mike Ashley days and built that department “from nothing”, he says. “That’s really helped me understand how to business-plan and build a team.”

Ameobi has been part of the Premier League’s Black participants advisory group since it formed in 2019. Its successes include implementing the No Room for Racism action plan in 2021 – this weekend marks the second of two match rounds this month dedicated to the initiative – and the pilot launch of the player to executive pathways scheme in 2022. That is designed to help those from underrepresented groups into executive roles and Ameobi was part of the initial seven-strong cohort.

“It gave me a really good framework,” he says. “It took us through, at a very high level, what the different streams of executive work look like, whether that’s finance, brand, the future of working, how organisations work. That gave me a little bit of confidence, if I’m honest, more than anything, to go: ‘Actually, a lot of the things I’m already doing translate into that world.’

“One of the biggest things was working in a pressurised environment, which is what I’ve done for 20 years, and learning how that can really help you in those moments when things get a little squirrelly, when they aren’t going as planned.”

Completing the programme fuelled a fire in Ameobi that had flickered for two decades. In November 2006, having struggled with a hip injury for several years, he had career-saving surgery. The resulting time out was “the start of my thought process beyond the football pitch”. Whether he would return to playing at his previous level, or at all, was uncertain. So, Ameobi’s thoughts turned to: “How can I best be useful to my teammates and this football club? That’s when the cog started turning.

“Football wasn’t always going to be there so who am I outside of the footballer? There were some really hard questions I had to answer during a very, very difficult time. There were some dark moments.”

Ameobi navigated those moments with the help of a psychologist and by leaning on his family. “I’ve always been fascinated with leadership, right from an early age,” he says. “My father was a leader of a church and he’s been the biggest influence of my life when it comes to leadership and how to lead well.”

He cites the late Sir Bobby Robson, who gave him his professional debut aged 18, and Alan Pardew, who managed him at Newcastle and Crystal Palace, as footballing influences. The former “still to this day has such an impact on the way I think about the game, the way I think about the person more than what you’re doing”, while the latter “used me to channel my leadership within the dressing room and wider squad to help him deal with some of the issues we were going through”.

Ameobi holds a master’s in strategic leadership and it is obvious at an event to celebrate the supporters’ group The Black Geordie Club that he is a natural with people. There is a presence to him, a warmth, that puts others at ease.

He is unrelenting in wanting to use his position to improve society. He grew up watching Andy Cole at Newcastle and memories of Tino Asprilla’s 1997 Champions League hat-trick against Barcelona bring the broadest of smiles. “That was the moment where time stood still. It was almost like I said to myself: ‘What’s wrong with you, Shola? You should be giving everything to be there.’

“You need to feel like you belong, you need to see yourself there, and that is so important when it comes to inspiration and people who aspire to be great in whatever sphere.

“It’s so important those who are in those positions give back and share their experience and share what helped them get to where they’re at. Being a young Black kid who has walked the walk throughout the club, from youth team to first team and now into this executive role, I want to show anything’s possible for anybody.

“Part of the reason I want to continue to push myself is because, yes, I want to be successful, but I want to show that anything’s possible if you’ve got the hunger, irrespective of what you look like and where you come from. I want to be an inspiration to the next generation.”
