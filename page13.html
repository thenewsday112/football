#192. An unmanageable club? Carrick the latest to suffer in Manchester United’s faded grandeur

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Only Matt Busby and Alex Ferguson have truly succeeded in the Old Trafford hot seat with each casting uneasy shadows

Should any football club fit the Great Man theory of history it is Manchester United. In the past 100 years, only Matt Busby and Alex Ferguson have knocked the club into shape. A pair of 20th-century Scotsmen, born 15 miles apart in Bellshill and Govan respectively, found the key. Otherwise, United has been a managerial dustbin, Michael Carrick the latest likely lobbed on the pile.

Busby and Ferguson built the club around themselves, possessing the charisma to convince others that theirs was the path to follow. Carrick, an insider, a club man, is falling victim to the same isolation as his predecessors in failure. Bruno Fernandes and Lisandro Martínez going off-piste in TV interviews to depth charge Carrick were actually following club tradition. With glamour comes decadence, the faded grandeur that has so often chased a United manager out the door.

Beyond Busby and Ferguson, each United manager who also served felt the lash of desperation of a falling giant. Each of their defenestrations has featured an infighting that would not have been out of place in ancient Rome. Favouritism, nepotism, self-interest, entitlement, complacency and sentimentality have been the betrayal of a legend, to use the title of Michael Crick’s and David Smith’s “j’accuse” book from 1989. Living with the legend and influence of those two managers leads the list of difficulties.

United fans know such history too well. “You lousy bastards, you let him down,” Brian Kidd told teammates when the bullet came for Wilf McGuinness, Busby’s immediate successor, in December 1970. Busby himself had done the deed. A December 1972 meeting at Selhurst Park with Tommy Docherty as Crystal Palace beat United 5-0 hastened the end for Frank O’Farrell.

Docherty’s turn came. His sacking in 1977 after an affair with the physio’s wife was announced by the club secretary, Les Olive, from behind the oak door of the chairman Louis Edwards’ Cheshire mansion. Dave Sexton was sacked in 1981 despite winning his last seven matches. Falling attendances at “Cold Trafford” did for “Whispering Dave”. Ron Atkinson turned his November 1986 sacking into a party in the office that also housed the club sunbed. Big Ron’s parting gift was the hangovers that greeted Ferguson’s first day in charge.

Ferguson’s predecessors had been intimidated by the fog of pipe smoke wafting down Old Trafford’s corridors. O’Farrell had needed to ask Busby if the former manager could vacate the manager’s office. “A fantasy world” is how O’Farrell described Busby’s coterie of gossipy former players who teed off with “the old man” at Davyhulme golf course. “There was no reality in the myth,” the Corkman dubbed “O’Failure” said in 1991.

Busby’s indulgence of George Best, careering towards oblivion, created problems O’Farrell found unsolvable. Docherty showed the political wiles to jettison Best in January 1974 but also shelling Bobby Charlton, Denis Law and Paddy Crerand created enemies. Falling in love with Mary Brown, with whom Docherty remained for the rest of his life, was enough for a boardroom moral majority to rid themselves of their turbulent manager.

Ferguson’s successors must deal with his continued presence, the face in the crowd photographers and TV directors seek. His patronage of Malcolm Glazer’s 2005 takeover is a dubious legacy dragging down those who have followed him. Ferguson mostly keeps his own counsel, a well-remunerated club ambassador until Sir Jim Ratcliffe cancelled a £2m annual stipend in 2024. There have been slips. “You should always start with your best players,” Ferguson told the mixed martial arts fighter Khabib Nurmagomedov in 2021 when Ole Gunnar Solskjær had dropped Cristiano Ronaldo, loose talk escaping to social media.

Ferguson’s part in Ronaldo’s disastrous return to Old Trafford evidenced continuing back-channel influence, echoing Busby and Best. Carrick, during his press conference last Friday, mentioned his chats with his former boss. The omission of Fernandes for the Bournemouth game, left to sulk on the bench in club tracksuit, was classic Fergie politics.

A Ferguson facsimile is a fool’s errand. Carrick might have learned the lessons dished out to Solskjær, to whom he was assistant. After refusing to use Ferguson’s parking space and misty-eyed chat of “the boss”, nobody was buying the affable, gentle Norwegian cosplaying as a Glaswegian hard man.

Kick off your evenings with the Guardian's take on the world of football

Not that an injection of the alpha male has been much more use. Time has been kinder to the Louis van Gaal and José Mourinho regimes as subsequent failures pile up, but both failed to embrace the expectations of attacking football.

Not that other club traditions were avoided. The subterfuge that brought in Mourinho was as dastardly as any of the post-Busby knifings. “People know him as a larger-than-life character,” the executive vice-chairman Ed Woodward said on Van Gaal’s 2014 appointment. “My wife had to find out when she was in the lift with Woodward’s family,” Van Gaal said of finding, in the haze of celebrating the 2016 FA Cup final victory, that he was being replaced. His former Barcelona assistant had successfully lobbied Woodward. Attempts have been made by the Glazers’ operatives and, since December 2023, Ratcliffe’s Ineos, to corporatise and professionalise but United’s innate excess and anarchy keeps overtaking rationale.

Using the means of a Netflix special, Mourinho has retrofitted his part in the story, he and Ferguson revealing together the fact he turned United down in 2013. The inference is that if Mourinho had arrived then all would have been roses. It is an unconvincing attempt at airbrushing both men’s reputations. Where does that leave David Moyes, the immediate successor painted as afterthought? In July 2013, Ferguson was celebrated as kingmaker, Moyes the rightful heir.

Moyes’s reputation is another enhanced by failures that followed, including Mourinho’s grumpy, fractious tenure. When emerging reluctantly from his Lowry Hotel bolthole, the Portuguese reduced himself to mocking club history with “football heritage” rants. Such a scorched-earth policy was echoed by Ruben Amorim, while Erik ten Hag’s retention by Ineos in summer 2024 was as confusing to Ten Hag as to everyone else.

An unmanageable club? Busby and Ferguson showed it can be done, though the chance of another empire recedes the longer the vanity, plotting and self-interest that personify United continues. And for that, Busby and Ferguson deserve a share of the blame.

#246. La principal plataforma china de contenidos, televisión e internet retira de su parrilla el Racing-Real Madrid

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

'Migu' cuenta con más de 400 millones de suscriptores

Migu, la principal y gigantesca plataforma china de contenidos, televisión e internet, que cuenta con más de 400 millones de suscriptores, ha retirado de su parrilla el partido Racing-Real Madrid del próximo domingo, correspondiente a la undécima jornada de Liga.

Los acontecimientos se siguen sucediendo desde que se conociese que la CMG, propietaria de los derechos de LaLiga en China, anunciaba que dejaba de retransmitir únicamente los partidos en los que participe Mbappé, después de que el francés reconociera que se ríe con los 'memes' que lo comparan con Mao Zedong. A partir de ahí, las consecuencias de la polémica se han ido sucediendo. Varias empresas chinas, entre ellas el grupo de bebidas Dongpeng y las compañías lácteas Mengniu y Milkground, anunciaron el cese de su cooperación comercial con el futbolista del Real Madrid. Una polémica que ha coincidido con el pleno del partido comunista chino, reunión a puerta cerrada que se prolongará hasta el jueves, y hasta donde ha llegado el 'caso Mbappé', un altercado que ha provocado que la imagen del Real Madrid en China esté seriamente dañada.

Mbappé tuvo que salir al paso de la polémica generada por sus declaraciones sobre China que han provocado un descomunal malestar en el país asiático. "Durante una entrevista reciente, reaccioné a una imagen mía en la que aparecía representado. Mi comentario fue desafortunado y ahora comprendo que ha herido a muchas personas en China y entre la comunidad china de todo el mundo", explicaba el futbolista francés en un comunicado firmado este martes 27 de octubre en París. El delantero madridista cerraba su comunicado con una disculpa y un mensaje de respeto hacia el país asiático: "Pido disculpas a todas las personas a las que mis palabras hayan ofendido. No era en absoluto mi intención. Siento un profundo respeto por el pueblo chino y su historia, y estoy sinceramente arrepentido".

Con este mensaje, Mbappé trataba de zanjar una polémica que había obligado intervenir a LaLiga. La patronal y su presidente, Javier Tebas, lamentaron el malestar causado y aseguraron que trabajaban con sus socios chinos para proteger la relación y evitar perjuicios a los clubes. "Entendemos el daño ocasionado y, en nombre de LALIGA, quiero trasladar nuestras sinceras disculpas al pueblo chino", afirma Tebas en un mensaje publicado en su cuenta de X. El propio Javier Tebas dio otro paso más en su intención de enfriar la polémica creada por la repercusión que han tenido en China las declaraciones de Kylian Mbappé. El presidente de LaLiga, tras pedir disculpas al pueblo chino tanto con con un mensaje en su cuenta de X como con un comunicado en la web de LaLiga, publicó un mensaje posterior, acompañado de una foto, de la reunión mantenida con el embajador de China en España.

#235. Man Utd stick with Carrick - inside a turbulent 48 hours at Old Trafford

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

An emotional Michael Carrick was cheered as he walked around the pitch after Sunday's defeat at Old Trafford

When Michael Carrick disappeared down the tunnel after Manchester United's loss to Bournemouth at Old Trafford on Sunday, the support from fans for a legendary player turned to hostility and anger at the current squad.

The atmosphere afterwards was said to be emotional.

Carrick was the last to leave the stadium and, as usual, patiently posed for selfies and signed shirts for the fans who remained on what had been a foul day weather-wise, and an awful one on the pitch.

Asked in his post-match news conference if he expected to be in charge for Saturday's trip to Chelsea, Carrick said: "I'm not expecting anything in particular, to be honest. I'm carrying on as normal."

Few felt that was sustainable. Even Carrick's coaching team wondered whether they would survive.

United have decided Carrick and his players will have the chance to turn the present situation around. No timescale has been put on this, but Carrick will be in charge at Chelsea, with his team 16th in the table.

They have a crucial Champions League match against Roma three days later, then a Premier League encounter with in-form Aston Villa before a two-week international break.

No-one from Manchester United is saying so, but that would be the most obvious time to decide if a change of coach is required.

On Monday morning, when it was widely anticipated an announcement terminating Carrick's tenure would be made, a senior figure at United said calmness and a clear head would be needed to navigate the turbulence around Old Trafford.

Carrick had been treading water since the crushing 4-1 defeat at Como on 21 October - one of the worst European nights in club history.

Respected United fan and United We Stand fanzine editor Andy Mitten, who interviewed chief executive Omar Berrada on behalf of the club last June, described the performance as "an affront to the name of Manchester United".

In addition to the catastrophic nature of the performance, captain Bruno Fernandes publicly questioned Carrick's tactics and the inability of his coaches to spot what was going wrong.

Fernandes' comments came a week after defender Lisandro Martinez said he had expected more investment in the squad and - turning attention to Jason Wilcox - "more from our technical director".

It painted a picture of a squad out of control, posing an immediate dilemma for the United hierarchy.

Feeling it would look reactionary and impulsive to remove Carrick based on the negativity of one game, United stuck with the 45-year-old for the Bournemouth match - only for that to turn sour thanks to a performance that, arguably, was worse than the one in Italy.

Fernandes played no part and was dropped to the bench. Afterwards, the Portugal star liked an Instagram post which included the caption: "Never push a loyal person to the point where they no longer care".

Late on Sunday night, BBC Sport was led to believe a final decision to sack Carrick had not been made, and the judgement would stretch into Monday.

There was no decision then, either. The United players who started against Bournemouth were given the day off but bizarrely, Fernandes and Carrick were both at Carrington. Fernandes was having pre-planned treatment while Carrick watched a closed doors Under-21s game.

Also at the training ground were Berrada and Wilcox, discussing what the immediate future should look like. Sources say it is inconceivable minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe and the Glazer family would not have been part of those discussions.

It was early evening before it became clear there would be no update on Carrick's future. His arrival at Carrington at 07:30 GMT on Tuesday was a sign he would take training as normal and, along with Fernandes and several other players, Carrick signed autographs and posed for photographs with a group of fans invited to the training ground that day.

Four hours later, United sources confirmed he would be in charge at Stamford Bridge.

Players were jeered by the crowd as they left the pitch after the loss to Bournemouth

The current predicament at the club is seen as not being entirely Carrick's fault.

Senior figures at the club feel United's players - a squad they think are capable of qualifying for the Champions League - are underperforming badly and should shoulder some of the blame.

There does seem to be a general desire to help Carrick out. One player told BBC Sport: "The feeling is that we all want to put it right. Players, staff, everyone."

It is not known if the "we" includes Fernandes, and BBC Sport has not been made aware of what, if any, conversations have taken place between the captain and his boss.

Obviously, it is easier to get rid of one manager and his backroom team than an entire squad of players - and if results do not turn around, Carrick will pay the price.

One former senior United official told BBC Sport: "Ordinarily, Michael would have gone. The last two performances were unacceptable and team discipline is broken. Not doing it is fascinating and risky."

Cynics may argue the main reason Carrick is still in a job is because of the inability to secure a replacement before the Chelsea game. Additionally, some of the coaches current Under-18s boss Darren Fletcher called on to help him during his spell as interim manager in January are now part of the senior set-up.

As highlighted by BBC Sport on Monday, Eddie Howe and Julian Nagelsmann were among the options explored by United as they weighed up whether to make a change. Any decision to appoint Howe would trigger a multi-million pound compensation payment from his former club Newcastle. Some observers think compensation payments may be a factor in United's reluctance to make a change, given the sums that have previously gone on hiring and firing coaches.

BBC Sport has also been told former Chelsea boss Antonio Conte is willing to have a conversation about the job, although there has been no direct contact.

That trio are all out of work - as is Kieran McKenna, who worked with Carrick as part of Ole Gunnar Solskjaer's coaching team at United and then left for Ipswich.

Aston Villa's Unai Emery also has strong backing at United, but the club are realistic about their chances of tempting the Spaniard from Villa Park.

Manchester United chief executive Omar Berrada (left) and director of football Jason Wilcox watched the Bournemouth match from the stands

Wilcox's future has also come under intense scrutiny.

One source close to United feels it is inevitable the former Blackburn and Leeds player will eventually lose his job if a third managerial change in as many seasons is made.

There is sympathy with Wilcox, who did not set the summer transfer budget. Others say Ratcliffe has provided significant funds for transfers since he took charge of football affairs in early 2024, as well as injecting Â£300m of his own money into the club.

While disgruntled fans may point to the recruitment of teams such as Brentford and Brighton as proof good players can be signed without paying astronomical fees, options for bigger clubs such as United were said to be scarce during the summer window.

Nevertheless, it has never been explained why it was decided to focus on bolstering the midfield last summer, rather than address clear personnel issues at left-back and central striker. Injuries to Luke Shaw and Benjamin Sesko have made that issue even more acute.

Another senior figure in the game told BBC Sport both Wilcox and Berrada may be vulnerable, suggesting Ratcliffe could be considering a wider overhaul of senior staff.

For now, though, minds are focused on Stamford Bridge, with Carrick due to speak to the media on Thursday.

#199. Are Celtic any nearer Ferrari form a year after Rodgers exit?

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Martin O'Neill appears in good spirits despite the stormy weather

One year ago, Brendan Rodgers resigned as Celtic manager, with Martin O'Neill immediately taking the reigns in what the Scottish champions thought would be an interim period in charge.

Twelve months on, O'Neill is still there, albeit over two spells with 33 dismal days under Wilfried Nancy squeezed in between.

So where are Celtic at in terms of "moving forward", that cliched business term that has seeped into football parlance?

Shortly before his exit, Rodgers described the Celtic squad as "a Honda Civic rather than a Ferrari" as he bemoaned transfer spending, but how much longer will it be steered by his 74-year-old successor?

O'Neill on Johnston injury, Celtic milestone & No.1 battle

O'Neill had admitted he considered his position during the last international break, but he showed no signs of being ready to walk away when addressing the media before Wednesday's meeting with Dundee United.

Asked how he looked back on his latest year in charge, he replied: "Obviously with a lot of satisfaction and secretly probably delighted to get the call and happy with the results since. So it feels like a long year. Very long.

"The club has done more for me than I've done for the club in that sense. Seriously, it's rejuvenated me in many aspects."

Previous to his return, O'Neill had been "semi-enjoying my life watching the game from afar" and "talking nonsense most of the time" as a football pundit.

However, he was delighted with the opportunity to return to "a game that I absolutely love, will love forever".

One year of O'Neill - saviour or sticking plaster?

Rodgers' second two-year spell as Celtic manager came to an end after a 3-1 defeat at Hearts that left his side eight points behind the Edinburgh side after nine games.

They had lost two of those nine, scoring 12 goals for the loss of eight.

Despite Hearts retaining their lead until the final day of the season, Rodgers' fellow Northern Irishman would turn it round in the end with a dramatic victory over their title rivals at Celtic Park to secure their 56th league title and fifth in a row.

This season, O'Neill's side have lost only one of their 10 league games, scoring 25 times for the concession of six, to lead Rangers in the title race.

On the surface, 16 wins in 17 league games since the closing stages of last season looks like progress.

As Celtic podcaster Tino Callaghan told the BBC's Scottish Football Podcast: "On the last day of the season - 16 May - everything did turn out fine and dandy.

"Currently, things are six out of 10 at Celtic. We're four points clear at the top of the league this week's midweek card can see us extend that lead, but things are far from correct in terms of the evolution of the club and the progress of the club."

O'Neill's win percentage of nearly 75% in his latest spell is similar to his club record level during his trophy-laden five years in charge to 2005.

However, the recent 7-1 thrashing of Hearts apart, performances this season have largely left the Celtic support underwhelmed, while it is results in Europe - and two defeats in a week by city rivals Rangers - that have caused most alarm.

When Rodgers left, Celtic had won eight games, drawn four and lost four overall at the start of last season.

This season, they have won 11 and lost six, but on their way to a Champions League exit and to the foot of the Europa League after three games, they have suffered heavy defeats against LASK, Ferencvaros and Benfica.

"O'Neill domestically is more than capable," Callaghan said. "But the Celtic squad is woefully inept when it comes to European football and you just need to have a glance at that for evidence when you look at the bench on those European nights."

That led former Celtic striker Chris Sutton to describe the side currently under his former manager as flat-track bullies only able to dominate in Scottish football.

"We're just falling way, way behind," Callaghan agreed. "But it wasn't that long ago where we were competing with Bayern Munich and beating Leipzig and pulling off some really decent results."

Striker Camilo Duran and midfielder Mika Baur have proved shrewd acquisitions

Last season was also marked by a damaging split between the board and sections of the support unhappy at the level of investment in the squad and the general running of the club.

The two sides united for last season's victorious climax to the season, but the splits have re-emerged.

"Ultimately it always comes down to recruitment, doesn't it?" Callaghan said. "I hear folks talking about entitled Celtic fans, but millions of pounds in the bank is no use unless you're spending it.

"It's about spending more effectively and it's spending certainly more efficiently and sooner within the window.

"The start of the window was exciting. Camilo Duran is a brilliant player. As good a signing as we've made in some time, Mika Baur the same.

"But the defensive midfield has been a serious issue. We haven't signed anyone there and you're now starting to feel sorry for Callum McGregor - he's been hung out to dry."

Daily Record football writer Michael Gannon agreed that O'Neill had been used as a human shield by the board.

"I feel for him a little bit because he's the only person at Celtic who fronts up and faces questions on a regular basis," he said.

"A lot of the questions, like where is the full-time chairman, where's the head of recruitment, where's the director of football, what's happened with the Green Brigade bans and fan problems, are not really for him to answer."

Callaghan appears to speak for many fans for whom O'Neill still feels like a stop-gap "holding position for all concerned".

"But, within that year, he's won a league and he's won a Scottish Cup as well, so not too bad," he said.

"I don't see him staying beyond this year. I think from his point of view, European football at Celtic became a thing again under O'Neill and I do think part of his decision-making in the summer to return will have been the prospect of a final chance at the Champions League, final crack at the big time.

"And he'll have been as devastated as anyone that we fell short against LASK. And I do think there's still a bit of a hangover amongst the players from that one."

After a poor start to the Europa League, will O'Neill now make finishing on a high domestically his priority?

"He will be conscious of his legacy whether he says so or otherwise and I think, whatever happens, he'll want to certainly move on this summer, if that is the case, with another league title under his belt," Gallacher added.

#153. Arise, Sir Harry? Downing Street hints at Kane knighthood after Ballon d’Or win

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Andy Burnham sends congratulations to England captain

Bayern player is first Englishman to win award since 2001

Downing Street has hinted that Harry Kane deserves a knighthood, ­praising the England captain’s “extraordinary achievement” as the first English winner of the Ballon d’Or in 25 years.

The prime minister’s spokesperson said Andy Burnham sent praise and congratulations to the England captain, who was announced as the men’s winner of football’s most prestigious individual award at a ceremony on Monday night.

No 10 said it hoped it would be the prelude to a trophy to celebrate with England “before too long” – alluding to hopes of an England win when the country co-hosts the European Championship in 2028 with Scotland, Wales and the Republic of Ireland.

The Bayern Munich player is the first Englishman to win the award since Michael Owen in 2001.

“The PM would like to congratulate Harry Kane on winning the Ballon d’Or,” the spokesperson said. “To be the first Englishman to win it in 25 years is an extraordinary achievement, and what a moment to do it here at home.

“Seventy years after Sir Stanley Matthews won the inaugural award, it caps a remarkable season and Harry continues to set a brilliant example to young players across the country, and the PM knows the whole nation will be incredibly proud of him. And, hopefully, there’s another trophy to celebrate with England before too long.”

Asked whether Burnham would like to see Kane receive a knighthood, she said: “I think everyone would agree, Harry Kane has given an enormous amount to English football, and this is a very special moment for him and the country, but, as ever, I’m not going to pre-empt the honours process.”

Kane received the award at a ceremony at the London Palladium on Monday night, beating Kylian Mbappé of Real Madrid and Barcelona’s Lamine Yamal. He is the fifth English player to win the award, the list also including Kevin Keegan and Sir Bobby Charlton.

Burnham cannot decide whether to hand Kane a knighthood, but ministers and departments can make recommendations to the honours committee, as can private individuals. The sport committee includes the presenter Sue Barker and the chief executive of the Football Foundation, Robert Sullivan.

Kane holds an MBE, awarded after England’s success in reaching the semi-finals in the 2018 World Cup. Players are often not awarded knighthoods until after retirement. The former England captain Sir David Beckham received his knighthood last year, as did the former England player and manager Sir Gareth Southgate.

Before those awards, the most recent was the former Scottish player and Liverpool manager Sir Kenny Dalglish. Owen, also a former Liverpool player, has not received a knighthood, and nor did Keegan, the former Newcastle player and manager who was also England’s captain and manager.

Nairobi — Malawi international Temwa Chawinga has made history by becoming the first African woman to finish in the top 10 of the prestigious Ballon d'Or Féminin rankings.

The Kansas City Current forward secured 10th place in the global rankings announced in London, surpassing the previous best finish by an African woman, Nigeria's Asisat Oshoala, who reached 12th place.

The milestone highlights Chawinga's remarkable rise in women's football, following a series of outstanding performances for her club in the United States' National Women's Soccer League (NWSL) and Malawi's national team, the Scorchers.

Chawinga has established herself as one of the most dangerous attacking players in the game, combining blistering pace, clinical finishing and an exceptional ability to find the back of the net.
