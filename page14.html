📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

El club ejecuta de forma anticipada la ampliación prevista en el contrato del extremo, una de las grandes promesas de la cantera valencianista

El Valencia ha asegurado la continuidad de David Otorbi hasta junio de 2031. El club ha ejecutado de manera anticipada la ampliación contractual que contemplaba el acuerdo firmado por el futbolista y blinda así a una de las principales promesas de su cantera.

La firma de la renovación se ha celebrado este martes en la Ciudad Deportiva de Paterna en un acto privado. Junto al futbolista han estado presentes sus padres y su representante, Nacho Gallart, además de responsables del club valencianista.

Otorbi, que sigue dando pasos en su progresión dentro de la estructura del Valencia, ha alternado durante esta temporada participaciones con el primer equipo y el Valencia Mestalla, consolidándose como uno de los jóvenes con mayor proyección de la entidad. La renovación supone una muestra de confianza del club en el desarrollo deportivo del atacante y refuerza su apuesta por el talento formado en la Academia.

El extremo valencianista se convirtió la pasada campaña en el jugador más joven en debutar con el primer equipo del Valencia en partido oficial y ha mantenido desde entonces una evolución que le ha permitido permanecer en la dinámica habitual de la primera plantilla.

El crecimiento de Otorbi también ha despertado interés fuera de España. MARCA informó recientemente de que el Newcastle había seguido de cerca las evoluciones del futbolista y que emisarios del club inglés estuvieron presentes en El Sardinero durante el encuentro entre el Valencia Mestalla y el Racing para observar al jugador. El seguimiento se enmarca dentro del interés habitual de clubes europeos por algunos de los jóvenes talentos con mayor proyección del fútbol español.

La renovación llega en un momento en el que el futbolista permanece a la espera de conocer el alcance exacto de la lesión muscular sufrida el pasado sábado frente al Villarreal. Otorbi tuvo que abandonar el terreno de juego tras notar molestias y está pendiente de las pruebas médicas y del correspondiente parte oficial del Valencia.

A falta de confirmación por parte del club, las primeras previsiones apuntan a un periodo de baja superior al mes, por lo que el extremo se perdería varios compromisos en un tramo importante de la temporada. #179. Alexia Putellas a worthy Ballon d’Or winner but the limited shortlist illustrates flaws

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Neglect of African football and a third win for the Spanish great does little to challenge narrative that the prize is reserved only for top nations’ players

After a four-year wait, Alexia Putellas is the queen of women’s football once again. Receiving the Ballon d’Or for a record‑equalling third time to continue Spain’s domination of the award, the 32-year-old reinforces her status as one of the greatest players of all time.

It was the sixth consecutive year in which the honour went to either Putellas or her international teammate Aitana Bonmatí, and it was symbolic of Putellas’s resurgence after her comeback from the anterior cruciate ligament injury she sustained in 2022, her refusal to surrender her career to that setback, and her majestic rise back to the top.

The midfielder’s competition‑leading 14 goal contributions – eight goals and six assists – in 11 Women’s Champions League appearances last season, helping Barcelona to lift the trophy, went a long way to her securing the most votes, as well as her starring role in Spain’s victory in the Nations League finals last December. Her new club, London City Lionesses, will rejoice in the prestige of having the Ballon d’Or winner in their squad but Putellas, who spent 14 years at Barcelona, made it clear whom she wanted to thank. As she said in her acceptance speech: “This prize is for Barcelona.”

Barcelona collected a clean sweep of the night’s bigger awards, with Cata Coll taking the goalkeeper prize, Pere Romeu the coach award and Vicky López the young player award, as well as Ewa Pajor finishing second behind Putellas for the main honour and Clara Serrajordi finishing second in the young player vote. It was almost a perfect night for the European champions, until their president, Joan Laporta, spoke bizarrely about his belief that their men’s team could win the Champions League this season, appearing to have eyes only for the men’s game despite specifically accepting the award for the women’s club of the year.

That slip aside, the glamorous ceremony at the London Palladium on Monday night passed without the sexist controversy that tarnished the early years after France Football belatedly added the women’s award for the first time in 2018. Progress has been made – the women’s game does now at least receive a prize for the same categories as the men, but the event still does not quite offer full equality. Specifically, there is still not quite parity in the lengths of shortlists. Why, for example, were 10 players nominated for the men’s young talent award, but only five for the women’s? It was the same for the goalkeeper awards.

The live show also demonstrated a disappointing lack of women’s players presenting prizes, aside from Lucy Bronze who was on stage to hand López her prize. Putellas receiving the Ballon d’Or Féminin from Michael Owen – rather than, perhaps Mia Hamm, Birgit Prinz or Sun Wen – perhaps served as a reminder that there are very few former winners of the women’s prize for the organisers to choose from – only Ada Hegerberg, Megan Rapinoe, Putellas and Bonmatí – because it was introduced so late.

Many fans will also have felt it was a shame that the dates covered by the relevant voting period – 3 August 2025 to 19 July 2026, effectively for last season – were based clearly on the conclusion of the men’s World Cup this summer, and therefore did not include this year’s rearranged Wafcon, which was held from 26 July to 16 August. Performances at that tournament will instead be taken into account for next year’s prizes. It meant, for example, that Cameroon’s Wafcon‑winning head coach, Valentine Nguele, was not nominated for the coach of the year award, and there was an overall dearth of African players recognised, aside from Temwa Chawinga becoming the first African player to finish in the women’s top 10. Arguably she deserved to be even higher.

Putellas was a very worthy winner of the main prize, although some will feel it is a missed opportunity for a player from one of the countries less likely to succeed in major international competitions to be recognised, given that the Women’s World Cup next year will inevitably ensure that the annual awards nights focus on whatever happens in Brazil. Poland’s Pajor, for example, finished second after a tremendous campaign with Barcelona but she feels unlikely to go one better next year, simply because of her nationality.

The same goes for Jamaica’s Khadija Shaw, who came third after firing Manchester City to their first domestic title in a decade – although the fact that she made the top three in spite of not playing Champions League football said a lot about how revered she is. Haiti’s Melchie Dumornay – the best player Moving the Goalposts had the pleasure of seeing play live in the 2025-26 season – finished fourth. Aged 23, her time may well come in the future, but it is most likely to be in an even-numbered year.

If Putellas continues her form, it would take a brave person to bet against her retaining her crown next year, as Spain target a successful defence of the world title. A humble role model for the sport, it is a privilege watching her grace the pitch with her deft touches, leadership and creativity and being a three-time Ballon d’Or winner is no less than her storied career deserves.

#147. Kylian Mbappé apologises after being dropped by Chinese brands for Mao Zedong comment

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Video appears to show Mao Zedong called a ‘dictator’

Dongpeng and Mengniu end deals with the player

Kylian Mbappé has apologised after the Real Madrid forward was dropped by major Chinese brands for apparently joking about Mao Zedong in a video that has gone viral. There were also signs of broader repercussions, with the state-run Chinese digital newspaper The Paper reporting that public television was suspending broadcasts of matches from La Liga.

The offending video shows Mbappé saying that the funniest internet meme he had seen involving him was of his face photoshopped on to that of “some guy – I think he’s a Chinese dictator”. This was an apparent reference to a meme of Mbappé superimposed on an image of Mao.

“My comment was ill-judged, and I now understand that it hurt many people in China and in Chinese communities around the world. I apologise to everyone my words have upset,” Mbappé said in a statement in French, Chinese and English.

Spain’s La Liga also apologised, saying it “deeply regrets the offence that Kylian Mbappé’s recent comments have caused among Chinese fans. These comments were made in a personal capacity and do not represent La Liga.” In China, jokes about Mao, the country’s founding leader who died in 1976 – and whose drastic policies are also blamed by historians for mass starvation and millions of deaths – remain taboo.

The beverage giant Dongpeng, which during this year’s World Cup ran adverts with AI-generated images of Mbappé consuming its sport drink, said it was terminating all cooperation with the France captain. “Our company firmly opposes and has a zero-tolerance policy towards any behaviour that offends national sentiments,” Dongpeng said in a statement.

Mengniu, China’s largest dairy producer, and cheese maker Milkground also said that they were cutting ties with Mbappé, who until now was hugely popular among Chinese fans with millions of social media followers. “Dongpeng Beverage ends cooperation with Mbappé” was the top-trending topic on Chinese social media site Weibo with 430m views.

One Weibo user said the “extremely serious wrong remarks … crossed the red line” and others urged Dongpeng to remove Mbappé’s image from their bottles. Dongqiudi, one of China’s biggest online football outlets, said Mbappé’s remarks were “offensive to China and the Chinese people”.

In an interview with the French sports newspaper L’Equipe last month, the 27-year-old Mbappé addressed a previous meme depicting him as a military dictator. “There’s a part of it that’s funny … but there is another part that’s a bit less funny because of the damage such people have caused throughout history,” he said.

China’s foreign ministry said it was aware of the reports regarding Mbappé, who on Monday finished runner-up to Harry Kane in the Ballon d’Or award for the world’s best player. “Words and actions that hurt the feelings of the Chinese people will naturally be met with unanimous rejection from all sectors of Chinese society,” spokesperson Guo Jiakun told a regular briefing.

Global sports stars who have commented on sensitive topics such as Taiwan, Tibet and Hong Kong have previously caused controversy in China and been dumped by sponsors there. The former NBA player Dwight Howard referred to Taiwan as a country in 2023 as he appeared in a video with the island’s then vice-president and current president, Lai Ching-te, sparking fury on Chinese social media. Beijing claims Taiwan is part of its territory and has threatened to use force to bring it under its control.

The Houston Rockets general manager, Daryl Morey, posted a tweet in 2019 supporting Hong Kong protests, leading to China suspending sponsorship deals with the NBA and pulling games off Chinese television for a year.

Fans in China made their feelings clear. “I used to really enjoy watching Mbappé play on the pitch,” Zhang Jingqi, 30, who works at a mall in Beijing, said. “But ever since this news broke yesterday I have lost that admiration, sorry to say.”

Reigning champion trainer heads to midweek fixture with five runners as he bids to snatch title lead from Pierre Ng

Caspar Fownes rates Sky Vino and Sky Cap solid top-three chances in their respective Class Three races at Wednesday night’s Sha Tin dirt meeting as he bids to snatch the trainers’ championship lead.

One win behind title leader Pierre Ng Pang-chi with 13 victories so far this term, Fownes has faith in Sky Vino despite the two-time all-weather winner drawing wide in barrier 11 in the Pyramid Hill Handicap (1,650m).

The consistent Vino Rosso gelding drops in grade after an encouraging seasonal reappearance, finishing third to Definitive in the Class Two Muk Min Ha Handicap (1,650m) on the dirt.

He notched his most recent all-weather triumph when overcoming gate 10 under Joao Moreira in May.

“Sky Vino handles the dirt very well. The 1,650m’s good, Vincent Ho rides him and he showed us last time he was competitive in Class Two, so he’s always a first-three player on that surface,” Fownes said.

Sky Cap is yet to win on the all-weather but comes off a solid fourth on the surface behind Kempes last start.

The four-time Happy Valley winner has drawn more favourably than Sky Vino in gate six in the Hebe Hill Handicap (1,200m), which shapes as a competitive contest.

“Sky Cap ran well last time against Hayesy’s horse who got beaten by [David] Hall’s horse,” Fownes said in reference to Kempes beating the $1.4 favourite Bustling City.

“He’s got the same horses again, but he’s probably improved another length and a half on that last run, so he could sneak into the top three.”

Keith Yeung Ming-lun will ride Sky Cap, while Fownes will also run Daily Trophy, Trophy Raider and Perfect Team on the nine-race card.

“It’s Daily Trophy’s last run for us, so he’s ready to go. He’s fit,” Fownes said.

#108. Angola: Angola Lead Cosafa Charge As FIFA Under-15 World Cup Enters Next Phase

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Angola have emerged as the standout COSAFA side at the inaugural FIFA Under-15 World Cup & Festival in Azerbaijan, winning all five of their group matches to remain in contention for the tournament's top honour as the competition moves into its second phase on Wednesday.

The Southern African nation was the only one of the 14 COSAFA representatives to finish top of its group, while South Africa, Zambia and Madagascar also produced impressive performances to earn places in the second tier.

The historic tournament in Baku features 189 national teams playing eight-a-side football, with matches lasting 40 minutes. The opening group stage was completed on Monday, with teams now divided into six tiers for the remainder of the competition.

Angola provided one of the best performances by an African team, winning all five Group J matches to finish with 15 points, scoring 17 goals and conceding six.

They opened with a 4-0 victory over Saudi Arabia before defeating China 3-2 and Japan 2-1, two results that underlined the quality of their campaign.

Victories over the Central African Republic and Solomon Islands completed a perfect record as Angola finished five points ahead of China and Japan.

They now enter Tier A, which brings together the 32 group winners to compete for the overall FIFA Under-15 World Cup title.

South Africa finished second in Group A with 10 points from five matches, winning three, drawing one and losing one.

They scored 25 goals, including an 8-0 victory over Maldives and a remarkable 13-1 triumph against Anguilla.

Their campaign also included a 3-2 win over France and a goalless draw with group winners Estonia, although a 2-1 defeat to Kosovo ultimately proved costly.

Zambia also impressed, winning four of their five Group X matches and scoring 22 goals. Their biggest victory was a 13-0 demolition of Montserrat, while they also defeated Tanzania 3-1 and Moldova 2-1. A 3-1 defeat to Uruguay meant they finished second on 12 points, level with the South Americans.

Madagascar were another regional success story, collecting 12 points in Group T with four victories, including a 2-0 win over COSAFA rivals Mozambique. Their only defeat was a narrow 1-0 loss to group winners Venezuela.

All three nations move into Tier B, where the group runners-up will compete for positions 33 to 64 overall. Malawi produced three victories in Group B, beating Chinese Taipei 3-2, Oman 3-1 and Suriname 3-1.

Defeats to Algeria and Argentina left the Junior Flames third with nine points, having scored 11 goals.

Mozambique recovered from their opening defeat against Madagascar to finish third in Group T with 10 points, recording three wins and a draw.

Their campaign included an entertaining 6-5 victory over Puerto Rico and a 1-1 draw against group winners Venezuela.

Namibia also registered three victories to finish third in Group AC with nine points. They started with an emphatic 8-0 success against São Tomé and Príncipe and added wins over Bermuda and the British Virgin Islands.

Zimbabwe collected six points in Group C with victories over Cambodia and Montenegro, finishing fourth behind the Republic of Ireland, England and Nicaragua.

Botswana also earned six points, defeating Papua New Guinea and Mongolia, but found the going tougher against Mali, Peru and Belarus in Group O.

Lesotho began with an encouraging 3-2 win over Gabon but lost their remaining three games to finish fourth in Group I.

Mauritius recorded a convincing 5-0 opening victory over Brunei Darussalam before taking one further point to finish fourth in Group R. Those four nations will compete in Tier D.

Comoros collected four points in Group X, while Eswatini earned three points in Group M. Seychelles secured one point from four games in the five-team Group V.

El Athletic está de enhorabuena y sus porteros de festejo.

Entre el curso 40-41, el del debut de Lezama, y 1980, que fue cuando se retiró Iribar, los tres aparecieron siempre en la portada del Athletic.

#317. Kane's rise from childhood keeper to the best in the world

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

PE teacher Mark Leadon said Kane was a "real team player" and "wasn't one to keep the ball if a pass was on"

The Bayern Munich striker continued his remarkable career journey on Monday night by becoming the first Englishman to win the Ballon d'Or for 25 years.

He is already England's joint record appearance maker and his country's record scorer with 89 goals in 124 appearances in an international career he started by opening his account 79 seconds into his debut.

It hasn't been the smoothest of journeys. From being rejected by Arsenal to loan spells in the Football League, the 33-year-old has had to defy the doubters throughout.

BBC Sport takes a look at the origin story of the England captain.

England's greatest international? Kane is now a serious contender

Kane ends England's 25-year Ballon d'Or wait - how he bucked the trend

When a six-year-old Harry Kane arrived for trials with Ridgeway Rovers in 1999, the youth side - who a generation earlier had produced David Beckham - thought they had found themselves a new goalkeeper.

Dave Bricknell, former Ridgeway Rovers coach: "We're doing a little shooting session, and I ask for a lad who can go in goal.

"This little boy puts his hand up and says: 'I'll go in goal.' We give him some gloves, and he performs heroics - makes some fantastic saves. He's tipping it round the post, tipping a couple over the bar.

"I'm thinking this is great, we've got a goalkeeper. And then this woman came up to me and said he's better on the pitch.

"We put him on pitch and he smacked goals in left, right and centre. So he ends up being our centre-forward."

Harry Kane with older brother Charlie and, right, with David Beckham and now wife Katie Goodland

Kane and his Ridgeway Rovers team-mates' goalscoring exploits were recorded in a notebook

Kane's prolific form for Ridgeway Rovers and his Waltham Forest district side caught the attention of professional scouts. Arsenal initially snapped up the youngster but decided not to keep him, while Watford also passed up the opportunity to sign Kane.

Trevor Bailey, Kane's manager at Waltham Forest: "Harry could have done nothing all game, but if that chance came up it was in the onion bag.

"He was always on the move up front. When he got a chance, guaranteed four out of five he was going to score or set someone up."

Alex Welsh, former academy coach at Arsenal: "I first met Harry in the academy and he was a very competent goalkeeper, but his real desire was to score goals.

"I am absolutely convinced that is the reason why he is such a clinical striker - he intuitively knows what the goalkeeper doesn't like. He shoots early when the goalkeeper's not set."

Bricknell: "I believe he was second top-scorer at Arsenal behind Benik Afobe, and then for some reason they decided at 11 years of age that they didn't want him any more."

Kane attended Larkswood Primary Academy before moving to Chingford Foundation School, where he was inevitably the star of the football team. But, as a dedicated student, Kane impressed his teachers across the board.

Mark Leadon, Kane's PE teacher: "Aged 11, Harry was technically very gifted, with a great first touch, and a very clever player. He always knew where to go, right from an early stage.

"He was also a very good cricketer - he opened the bowling from year seven to 10 and batted at four or five. But he loved football. He was never a prima donna, and he would always put a shift in.

"What came through more than anything else was his determination to succeed. That work ethic was clear."

Luke Robinson, Kane's childhood friend and team-mate: "The only thing on his mind was football.

"We used to have a laugh, a couple of games of Fifa, but he was always driven. He'd always keep himself to himself.

"He loved David Beckham, he came in with the Mohican, everyone was laughing but he loved it. He loved football, 24/7, he lived and breathed football."

Kane's old coach: He always had 'thirst' for goalscoring

It was Tottenham Hotspur who offered Kane hope after his earlier disappointments, but the future England captain was by no means the pick of the bunch for his age group.

Kane's desire to learn and improve endeared him to the Tottenham academy staff and he signed scholarship terms with the club on his 16th birthday in July 2009.

Alex Inglethorpe, former Tottenham youth-team coach: "When I joined Spurs as under-18 coach, Harry was 13.

"I'd like to say he was the gold medallist in the group and we'd all spotted he was going to be the fantastic player, but I'd be lying. He probably wasn't even on the podium.

"If there was something that endeared him to you it was that he was very, very low maintenance. It became fairly evident when Harry became a scholar he was someone who could finish.

"He was wonderfully demanding to coach because he always wanted to stay out and do extra. That obsession to improve is undoubtedly his greatest strength.

"It was probably around 16 he started having an impact on the youth team. We trusted him to come off the bench and have an impact - anywhere from right wing, number 10, centre-forward, sometimes in midfield."

Kane (centre) scored five goals during an 18-game loan spell at Leyton Orient in 2011

Kane went to League One Leyton Orient - making his professional debut in January 2011 at Rochdale, coming on for veteran striker Scott McGleish. Kane would score five times in nine starts.

McGleish: "Harry was not like other Premier League players, where you feel they're going to come in 'Billy Big Time'. He wanted the experience of learning what I call men's football.

"From the word go he was ready to fight for his place. In training, he was willing to just knuckle down and work hard.

"You could see in training and subsequently the matches he had ability and a desire to go further, but you're still looking like a young 17-year-old: very slight, still needing to physically develop, not the quickest but technically excellent."

After a few first-team games for Spurs, he went on loan in January 2012 to Championship strugglers Millwall. Kane's nine goals helped save Kenny Jackett's side from relegation.

Former Millwall defender Alan Dunne: "I've seen a lot of players come to the Den and freeze, because within five minutes they're being called names they've never heard before and they can't cope with that pressure.

"But it didn't faze him - he was a focused lad, he was confident in his ability."

Former Tottenham manager Tim Sherwood: "I used to watch Harry a lot at Millwall, and it was an intimidating atmosphere. I remember overhearing one of our scouts come and talk to Harry Redknapp - who was Spurs manager at the time - and say: 'We need to get Harry Kane out of Millwall, it's no good for him there.'

"I went straight in and said: 'This is the perfect atmosphere for him."

After making his Premier League debut for Tottenham in August 2012, Kane was sent on loan again. The 19-year-old only made three appearances in the top flight for Norwich because of injury, and then had a loan spell at Leicester where he and future England team-mate Jamie Vardy struggled for regular starts.

Kane (centre, pictured playing against Hearts in 2011) joined Tottenham as an 11-year-old and signed professional terms on his 16th birthday in 2009

After four loan spells in three seasons, Kane spent the entire 2013-14 campaign with Tottenham. But it was not until April, after Andre Villas-Boas had been sacked and replaced by Sherwood, that a 20-year-old Kane was handed his first Premier League start for Spurs, scoring his first top-flight goal in the process against Sunderland and also netting in the following two games against West Brom and Fulham.

Sherwood: "It didn't feel a gamble at all to give Harry that start. It was almost overdue to be honest.

"Roberto Soldado was in front of him in the team, and nobody wanted Roberto to do well more than me - but every single day in training Harry was outperforming him. He didn't let me down.

"Harry wanted to get to the top, and nothing was going to stop him achieving that because of the ability, desire and mentality he possesses.

"I was the biggest believer Harry Kane ever had, because I used to think he was a mix between Teddy Sheringham and Alan Shearer."

Kane became Spurs' all-time top scorer on 5 February 2023, passing Jimmy Greaves' old mark of 266

Kane never looked back after scoring 21 top-flight goals the following season - and earning his first England call-up in March 2015.

He ended up as the club's all-time top scorer with 280 goals in 435 appearances - and three Premier League Golden Boots, but no trophies

Then Manchester City boss Pep Guardiola, who tried to sign him, said in 2023: "What an exceptional player - the numbers and, more than the goals, the quality. Harry Kane remains one of the best strikers I have ever seen in my life."

Former Spurs team-mate Son Heung-min: "Leader, brother, legend.

"Since day one it has been a joy to play by your side. So many memories, amazing games and incredible goals together."

Then Tottenham boss Ange Postecoglou: "Harry was brilliant throughout. You want to work with the best players and he is a hell of a striker and I thought he could help me.

"He has left an indelible mark at this football club, will go down as one of the greats."

Harry Kane won the German double this season

The only thing missing from Kane's career CV was trophies - and in 2023 he joined German side Bayern Munich, where he has continued to impress.

He has scored 156 goals in 159 games for the club, won the Bundesliga Golden Boot three times and won two German titles and a German Cup. Now he has been named the best player in the world.

Former Bayern defender Lothaur Matthaus: "Kane has reinvented the centre-forward game, as Manuel Neuer did with goalkeepers.

"I don't see a better striker in the world right now. Not just because of his goals, but because of his versatility and the positions he plays.

"Kane also demonstrates qualities as a passer, dribbler, and tackler. He is simultaneously a goalscorer, playmaker, box-to-box player, and tackler."

Ex-England striker Alan Shearer, speaking this summer: "To me he looks fitter and stronger than before.

"He's come into this World Cup off the back of a brilliant campaign for Bayern Munich and looks very sharp and very confident - as you would do after scoring 61 goals in one season. Those numbers are just ridiculous."

BBC pundit Chris Sutton: "He has to be in that bracket when you talk about the world's best. Harry Kane has instincts that are simply uncoachable."
