#79. Ryan Moore, James McDonald and Zac Purton headline stellar International Jockeys’ Championship line-up

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

William Buick, Mickael Barzalona, Saffie Osborne, Dylan Browne McMonagle, Christophe Lemaire and Rachel King complete the overseas cast

International stars Ryan Moore, James McDonald and Joao Moreira, plus nine-time Hong Kong champion Zac Purton, headline a stellar line-up for December’s Longines International Jockeys’ Championship (IJC) at Happy Valley.

The current top five in the Longines World’s Best Jockey standings – Moore, McDonald, William Buick, Mickael Barzalona and Purton – will contest the world’s most prestigious jockey challenge, while rising British star Saffie Osborne will make her IJC debut.

Moreira, the winner of the 2012 IJC, will make his 11th appearance this year, with Irish champion Dylan Browne McMonagle, French star Christophe Lemaire and Australian-based Rachel King adding further class to the line-up.

Purton is assured of his place as Hong Kong’s reigning champion jockey. The final two berths on the 12-rider roster will go to the city’s leading home-grown jockey and highest-ranked eligible rider in the championship standings at the cut-off date, which follows the Happy Valley meeting on November 25.

“The 2026 edition of the Longines International Jockeys’ Championship is shaping to be one of our best, featuring Ryan Moore, James McDonald, William Buick, Mickael Barzalona and our champion Zac Purton,” Andrew Harding, the Jockey Club’s executive director of racing, said.

“To have the current top five in the Longines World’s Best Jockey rankings taking part in the IJC says everything necessary about the quality of this year’s competition.

“I’m especially pleased to welcome Saffie Osborne for the first time. She is without a shadow of a doubt one of the best up-and-coming young riders anywhere in the world, and she captured the imagination of racing fans all over the world this year with her history-making victory in the Group One St Leger.

“To also have Irish champion jockey Dylan Browne McMonagle, as well as Rachel King, Christophe Lemaire and Joao Moreira, rounds out the international competitors perfectly.

“With this star-studded line-up and the iconic and spectacular Happy Valley racecourse as the setting, we have all the ingredients for a thrilling night of great sport.”

The elite riders will battle for a total bonus prize fund of HK$1 million across four races on December 9, with 12 points for a win, six points for second place and four points for third.

Rides are allocated with a model designed to make the contest competitive and to reduce the likelihood of individual riders being advantaged or disadvantaged by the draw.

Moore joined Purton, Douglas Whyte and Frankie Dettori on a record three IJC victories when successful last year.

Osborne was recently crowned winner of the 2026 World Pool Jockeys’ Championship on British Champions Day at Ascot.

The 24-year-old created history by becoming the first female jockey to win a British Classic last month with victory aboard Highwayman in the Group One St Leger.

She will ride on a short-term contract in Hong Kong for the first time from November 8 to December 23.

The Australian jockey, whose Hong Kong licence expires in February, lauded Raging Blizzard after the seven-year-old’s career-best triumph.

“There are two horses that, every season, they keep turning up for me. They don’t win very often but him and Beauty Joy, they just turn up every season and run against the best and I said to connections that ‘I think it’s his turn today’,” Avdulla said.

“I just thought it was the easiest race he’s been and Hally’s horses have been in good form, but today, carrying the 135lb, second up and 1,200m – I thought today was D-Day for him and we got the right run through and he was able to get the job done,” Avdulla said.

Raging Blizzard handed Size a record-extending fifth Premier Bowl success after the master trainer’s previous wins with the Avdulla-ridden Sight Success (2023), Hot King Prawn (2018), Mr Stunning (2017) and Rich Unicorn (2011).

Since his previous win in January last year, Raging Blizzard had been placed seven times – including three behind the world’s best racehorse, Ka Ying Rising.

“It’s a bit of justice for him – he’s tried so hard in all his races. He’s been unlucky running into some very nice horses constantly, but eventually, he’s rewarded for his consistency. So, It was quite satisfying to see him win this race,” Size said.

“It was probably a special event for a lot of the connections, so we’re quite happy and relieved for the horse to win a race and for the owners, who have been believing in him for such a long time.

“He’s got a lovely temperament, he’s in beautiful condition most of the time and he’s sound and he does very well. He’s a pleasure to work with. But just race day, he just lacks the extra couple of rating points to mix it with the big ones.”

More clashes with Ka Ying Rising loom when Raging Blizzard progresses to the Group Two Jockey Club Sprint (1,200m) next month and December’s Group One Hong Kong Sprint (1,200m).

“He’s got no options, so he’ll go there and it’s a stepping stone to the second Sunday in December,” Size said.

Avdulla produced a top ride to bag the first leg of his double, guiding Australian import Pocketing to his first Hong Kong win in the Class Three Lukfook Jewellery Miss Hong Kong Love Yourself Collection Handicap (2,000m).

The David Hayes-trained gelding stormed home from midfield on the fence approaching the turn to beat Big Return by a neck.

“The last run was very good. He ran home strongly for fourth and then today he got a great ride from Brenton and the 2,000m, good barrier, good ride – he won. I’m hoping now over distance and fit, he can do it again,” Hayes said.

“[Avdulla] was very happy when the mid-race pressure came on that allowed the race to suit the horses from midfield and back – they went far too fast down the back straight.”

A Group Two winner pre-import, Pocketing was Hayes’ first Hong Kong winner for Yulong Investments owner Zhang Yuesheng.

“I’ve trained winners in Australia for him. He’s a lovely man and a huge owner,” Hayes said.

“I’ve got another horse for him that will be racing in one month’s time, so to have a couple of nice horses for the big owner, is a thrill.”

Elsewhere on Sunday’s card, injury-plagued galloper Megastar Heart backed up his stunning first-up win with a dominant success in class record time.

The Australian import made it back-to-back Class Three victories by two lengths under Luke Ferraris, boosting trainer Caspar Fownes’ confidence in him reaching a rating of 100 in the future.

“Like I said the last time he won, he’s been one of the toughest horses I’ve had to train through my career, so for us we get quite emotional and it’s fulfilling to get these types of horses to perform,” Fownes said.

“I’m confident he’s going to get to triple figures but he could be even better than that going forward, if he stays sound. He’s won it pretty soft, he’s run good time and he’s just going to keep improving.

“His next race will be on the 14th of November in the Panasonic Cup. If things stay the way they are, he’s going to be a live chance in Class Two which is awesome.”

Megastar Heart capped a double for Fownes after he struck earlier with Tycoon Express. He sits second in the championship standings on 13 wins – one behind leader Pierre Ng Pang-chi.

Trainer Ricky Yiu Poon-fai also recorded a double at Sha Tin courtesy of Chill Easy and Packing Glory. Chill Easy was the second leg of a brace for jockey Jerry Chau Chun-lok, who won earlier on Danny Shum Chap-shing’s debutant Happy Pegasus.
