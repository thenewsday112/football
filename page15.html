#251. Kane’s Ballon d’Or triumph a glorious riposte to celebrity fawning of ceremony | Barney Ronay

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The England captain spoke movingly on a night of deserving winners and unintentional hilarity in London

Well, there’s something new. Football: now available in new, genuinely heartwarming flavour.

There was a lovely moment at London’s Palladium theatre on Monday, with the clock creeping towards 11pm at the end of a startlingly shrill and bloated evening of never‑ending awards and never-ending award‑related excitement.

Not just for Harry Kane, who is now the 2026 men’s Ballon d’Or winner, and in the process placed indelibly in that lineage of elite footballers. But also, frankly, for anyone watching, as Kane stood on stage gazing a little shyly at his golden orb, and kicked off his speech by thanking everyone, paying tribute to everyone, everywhere, then finally cracking into tears and a strangled croak as he thanked his parents for sacrifices made, his wife for always being there, and even, in slightly jarring vibe-shift from the warm honeyed tone, José Mourinho.

Even Kane’s biggest advocates will rarely claim their man is one of a kind, sui generis, in the mode of the Ronaldo-Messi godhead. But Kane is exactly this in his own admirably humble way.

Not just an exceptional footballer, the best, most deserving men’s player in the world this year, a European golden boot, scorer of 89 goals since August 2025, Bundesliga champion, World Cup semi-final captain, model of extreme professionalism and care. He’s also quite clearly the nicest man, if not just in football, then possibly the world.

Or at least it felt like that as he walked off in that very dear and tender way, the centre of the world in that moment, but also just keen to get off the stage and let everyone get on with clearing up.

It would be wrong to say Kane’s victory in the final vote of the night redeemed the grotesqueness of the ceremonies itself. There was a roomful of hugely deserving winners here. But Kane hits particularly hard.

And the Ballon d’Or is, let us be clear, a horrendous event, albeit a supremely popular horrendous event. There was the usual pomp around the ceremony, staged this year at a fancy theatre close to Oxford Circus.

This isn’t sport. It’s celebrity culture, red‑carpet fawning, the worship of images and people, and fame. Football isn’t supposed to be cocooned this way, to send the message that there are barriers and ropes and special people. Yeah, well, good luck with that.

The night kicked off with French Montana in spotless white tuxedo performing on an open-air double-decker bus. He definitely speaks, clearly, carried well, seemed enthusiastic. If it all goes south from here, the evidence suggests French Montana could, at the very least, get a job as a London tour bus operator.

Before long Thierry Henry and Kate Scott glided on stage, half an hour late, to add a little deft and easy charm to a furious schedule of pots and prizes.

And the awards came thick and fast. The Spain manager, Luis de la Fuente, was named coach of the year. “I hope in the future I will become in English better,” were his first words at the lectern, throwing his hat in the ring for a disastrous pension-padding six months at Manchester United two years from now.

There was a lovely moment as Burna Boy came on to present the Socrates award for charitable works to Bukayo Saka. He was asked if there were footballers who had inspired his love for the game. At which point he looked at Thierry and said, “definitely, one of them’s standing right there”, and of course he said it in his Burna Boy voice, looking Thierry deep in the eyes so smooth and deep, just almost an impossibly sexy moment.

The Ballon d’Or can be unintentionally hilarious at times such as these. There were just so, so many video tributes, so much excitingly portentous music. This is a stage where everyone happening at every moment is very clearly the most important thing ever to happen, superseded only by the next thing to happen on that stage, and then the thing after that.

The degree of fawning is startling at first, but glazed by familiarity before long. At one point Cristiano Ronaldo appeared very briefly in a fact-cut big-screen montage and the crowd shrieked a song, clutched its hand to its fevered throat, collapsed into the aisles in sheer priapic excitement.

Although by the time the original Ronaldo emerged onstage at 10.44pm, there was a sense of the genuinely vertiginous company Kane was about to join. Ronaldo read out the name inside the envelope, and the hall collapsed into cheers and yells and roars as Kane appeared onstage.

And yes the Ballon d’Or is a fundamentally silly thing on its own terms. It jars with the basic concept of team sport, promoting instead the opposite of sport, the opposite of team culture, the modern disease of fawning parasocial engagement with star names. In many ways the world would be a better place if the Ballon d’Or was simply abolished, as an example to all who pore over reels and clips and the imagined qualities of their celebrity paramours.

But it is also fascinating, unignorable, and endlessly moreish. All the more so this time. Kane as the Ballon d’Or 2026 is a genuinely fine, uplifting, instructive moment in the spotlight.

It is always tempting to talk about the journey these players make. Everyone has a backstory. Most footballers come from adversity. Kane has this, in his own way, 16 years into a professional career which has names such as Leyton Orient, Millwall, Leicester and Norwich on the timelines. He was unexceptional as a junior, didn’t stand out as particularly skilful or big or outstanding in his numbers. He played as a holding midfielder for a bit while Spurs decided what to do with him.

Kane worked relentlessly. He was Millwall young player of the year in 2012, fitting bookend to the Ballon. He made it because he simply refused to go away, to stop working, honed instead the deeper skills in his quiver, the purity of his shooting, the range of his passing long and short.

And Kane is exceptional in other ways. Without ever peacocking around promoting his brand, without really trying he remains the most likable, authentic, role‑model‑ish modern-day England player. For a man who never talks big or acts big he has a happy knack of becoming the key player wherever he goes. Perhaps Kane has become cool now. He has always been likable, with that mild-mannered air of a kindly Victorian chimney sweep with a hamster in his waistcoat watch pocket. In the Bayern years, Kane has become leaner, more glossed and handsome, jawline, tan, iconic Vampire hairline.

Mainly he expresses a common confusion about what talent really is. Talent isn’t simply moments. Talent is also application, focus, craft, the ability to work, to make others better.

And yet his elite career has also been oddly grudging. Somehow the idea has always been out there that Kane is undeserving of these accolades, despite his own mildness and humility, his obvious team qualities, his relentless delivery of goals, effort, leadership. It is a genuinely odd conclusion given the opposite is so manifestly true, that this is an entirely uplifting story of overachievement, of hard work and applied intelligence, as opposed to the kind of talent that just needs to be flame‑throwered in the right direction.

It is undeniably true that Kane’s levels can drop dramatically when he can’t perform at his full physical capacity. There have been moments where it has been tempting to wonder if he’s gone far enough as England’s captain in the past few years, if his body and his talent deserve a little care.

Well, that just shows how wrong you can be.

#346. A San Mamés a por el repóker

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Matarazzo logra por primera vez en LaLiga cuatro triunfos seguidos, su tope también en la Bundesliga, y el domingo buscará en Bilbao la mejor marca de su carrera

Con la lograda contra el Levante Pellegrino Matarazzó alcanzó por primera vez en LaLiga cuatro victorias seguidas, después de que la pasada temporada se quedase en tres, una marca que intentará mejorar el próximo domingo frente al Athletic de Bilbao en San Mamés. Ni que decir tiene que sumar los tres puntos sería la mejor ratificación al gran momento que viven el equipo txuriurdin y su técnico, que enlazaría por primera vez en su carrera el repóker de triunfos consecutivos..

Matarazzo tuvo hace poco más de una decena de meses un gran impacto en la Real y en LaLiga, al llegar con el equipo tan sólo dos puntos por encima de los puestos de descenso y con unas muy malas sensaciones que enseguida convirtió en buenas, al enlazar seis jornadas sin perder, entre ellas tres triunfos consecutivos ante Getafe, Barcelona y Celta.

Después su trayectoria se normalizó más, alternando victorias, empates y derrotas, hasta que logró el pase a la final de la Copa, ansiada por ser esta vez con público en la grada, no como la anterior de 2021, y esa decisiva cita y el título logrado en ella, con clasificación incluida para la Europa League, el otro objetivo del curso, hizo que la recta final ya poco importase.

LaLiga 2026-27 arrancó con las dos conocidas visitas a Betis y Real Madrid, comprobando que el triunfo era muy complicado de lograr, aunque en La Cartuja estuvo cerca, pero luego ha aprovechado una fase menos dura de calendario para hacerse con seis victorias en ocho jornadas, las cuatro últimas seguidas, una mejor racha que puede ampliar el domingo en San Mamés con otro éxito que sería muy valioso a todos los niveles.

Ganar en Bilbao permitiría a Matarazzo lograr su mejor marca de triunfos seguidos en su carrera como entrenador, ya que en sus distintas experiencias en la Bundesliga también llegó a cuatro, en la 2023-24 con el Hoffenheim, cuando superó a Heidenheim, Wolfsburgo, Colonia y Unión Berlín. El de Nueva Jersey tiene, por tanto, una motivación más para este derbi.

#163. Zimbabwe: Peter Ndlovu Replaces Mapeza As New Scottland FC Coach

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Warriors legend Peter Ndlovu will lead Scottland FC as the club's new head coach after Norman Mapeza was sacked for failing to help the side qualify for the CAF Champions League group stages.

Ndlovu, who served as the club's team manager, will take over on an interim basis and oversee the team for the remaining league matches.

The former Mamelodi Sundowns team manager holds a CAF A coaching licence, and this marks his first professional coaching job.

Danger Fourpence will assist Ndlovu from the club's junior team, while Knowledge Musona will also double up as a player-coach.

Scottland FC are currently second on the log with 61 points, eight behind leaders Hardrock FC, who sit at the top with 68 points.

However, the Mabvuku-based side have two games in hand against CAPS United and Chicken Inn.

Apart from the league, Scottland FC are also in the ZIFA Munhumutapa Cup semi-finals, where they are set to face Herentals.

Meanwhile, sources close to Scottland have indicated that Dynamos coach Aristica Cioaba is likely to take over the vacant job at the end of the season.

Apart from Cioaba, former Warriors coach Michael Nees is also linked with the job.

#310. Ranking England's Ballon d'Or winners - how does Kane compare?

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

English Ballon d'Or winners have been few and far between. Triumphs to be treasured, names never to be forgotten.

Harry Kane, England's great talisman and captain, is the first to claim football's grandest individual accolade in 25 years after a sensational campaign in Bayern Munich colours - and only the fifth Englishman to ever do so.

Before him Michael Owen, in 2001, was the first Three Lions star to take it since Kevin Keegan's successive triumphs 22 years earlier.

England World Cup winner Bobby Charlton did it in 1966, and that came after Stanley Matthews won the inaugural prize a decade earlier - handed what began life as the European Footballer of the Year award in 1956.

Others have come close - Billy Wright, Bobby Moore, Gary Lineker, David Beckham and Frank Lampard are all among the runners-up. Lucy Bronze, in 2019, and Beth Mead, 2022, also more recently finished second.

Of course, the award has gradually evolved into the global behemoth it is today.

The caveat with those earlier winners is that it was not a worldwide prize - until 1995 it was only open to European players, and only in 2007 did it expand to include all players from all clubs across the world.

However, that does not detract from the award-winning performances these stars produced - each enjoyed prize-worthy campaigns that caught voters' eyes across Europe and the world.

But who among the five to have held the coveted prize is England's greatest Ballon d'Or winner?

Only Brazil's Ronaldo, in 1997, was younger than Owen when he won the Ballon d'Or in 2001

We won't hold it against Michael Owen for suggesting he didn't know what the Ballon d'Or was when Gerard Houllier first rang to announce he'd won it.

And, in fairness, the then 22-year-old swotted up sufficiently by the time he received the award, reeling off a list of previous winners.

Owen in those days, even after his initial hamstring struggles, was explosive, electric - searing pace and acceleration complemented by the most ruthless of finishes. He became a renowned goalscorer in his teenage years and, by 2001, was one of the most sought-after youngsters in world football.

Owen's Ballon d'Or-winning year was one of iconic moments. He helped Liverpool to a treble, clinching the League Cup on penalties before beating Arsenal in the FA Cup final - Owen scored twice late on to turn the game in Liverpool's favour.

The leveller was an acrobatic and clinically taken strike inside the Arsenal box, the winner a devastating break in which the prolific striker darted away from Lee Dixon and Tony Adams before slotting past David Seaman with his left foot.

The forward then set up two of the goals as Houllier's side beat Alaves 5-4 in extra time in the Uefa Cup final, having earlier in the competition scored a famous double against Roma at the Stadio Olimpico that carried the Reds through.

He finished the season as Liverpool's top goalscorer with 24 in all competitions. Owen's most iconic performance that year, however, came for England against Germany, scoring a famous hat-trick in Munich.

Injuries meant Owen never maintained that peak for as long as he would have hoped. Indeed, only Brazilian great Ronaldo claimed a Ballon d'Or at a more tender age.

Blackpool's Stanley Matthews (left) was 41 when he won the inaugural award

Sir Stanley Matthews, first and oldest winner of the Ballon d'Or - a prize then decided by voters from 16 countries.

Twelve of the panel put the Blackpool winger on their ballot, with six placing him first - beating Alfredo di Stefano and Raymond Kopa, who competed in the inaugural European Cup final for Real Madrid and Reims, respectively. That alone shows how revered Matthews was.

Matthews, two months shy of his 42nd birthday when he received the award, led Blackpool to second in the English top flight. This was behind Manchester United's Busby Babes that season, though voters were likely influenced by his performance in the FA Cup final three years earlier. Then, the Tangerines beat Bolton 4-3 in a match dubbed the 'Matthews Final' and broadcast in cinemas.

In an era where following the game often came largely via radio and newspaper reports, Matthews' mythical status drew fans to stadiums in huge numbers, with curious onlookers keen for a glimpse of his magic whenever Blackpool came to town. He dazzled for England, too.

Matthews was born during the First World War and would remarkably continue playing for another nine years after his award, retiring aged 50. His longevity no doubt was a factor, but should not take away from his continued brilliance.

Matthews was famous the world over, lauded by fellow stars for his creativity and ingenuity at a time where greatness did not boil down to individual stats. A creator rather than a scorer.

He was innovative, too. Nicknamed 'The Wizard of the Dribble', he would perform stretching and breathing exercises, do extra training and take care of his diet - fasting and not drinking alcohol.

Most poignantly, he was one of football's great entertainers, described by Ballon d'Or founder Gabriel Hanot - who was thought to have preferred Di Stefano - as the sport's Charlie Chaplin.

Matthews was adored by a generation, an outside right who became an icon. He received the award in the modest surroundings of Blackpool Town Hall, a low-key affair but one that cemented his legacy.

Now, Harry Kane has produced one of the most sensational individual campaigns on record. You could argue that should place him higher, but those above him on this list have done some heavy lifting, too.

Looking at his output from the beginning of the 2025-26 season to the end of this summer's World Cup, the evaluation period for this year's award, Kane has delivered the most goals for club and country by any player in Europe's top five leagues since Lionel Messi's astonishing 2011-12 return.

The England skipper netted 61 times in 51 outings across all competitions for Bayern, winning a German league and cup double while also becoming the fastest player to hit a century of Bundesliga goals.

He also scored 12 times for his country, with six of those coming during England's run to the World Cup semi-finals.

The argument, of course, is that Kane is playing for a dominant Bayern side in a league they are heavy favourites to win each year.

The counter is the 33-year-old has redefined the centre-forward role - his incredible passing range and hold-up play making him an all-round playmaker.

Kane has been developing and maturing into one of England's all-time greats ever since those early loan spells at Leyton Orient and Millwall. His Premier League output for Tottenham was phenomenal season upon season, gradually adding assists to a prolific goal output.

In Munich, especially last season, Kane has just taken that to another level.

The question mark hanging over those winners pre-1995, when the award opened beyond European players, or even before 2007, when it became truly global, is whether there was a more worthy recipient sitting outside the current criteria.

Some may argue Igor Belanov was slightly upstaged by Diego Maradona in 1986, for example.

But 20 years earlier, few could dismiss the credentials of a man who pioneered the Three Lions' first and only World Cup triumph, Manchester United's Bobby Charlton.

A survivor of the Munich Air Disaster in 1958, Charlton and United went on to win the FA Cup in 1963 and English top flight two years later. By 1966, at the age of 28, Charlton was in his prime, heading into the World Cup on the back of 16 top-flight goals from midfield for United that season.

Not only was he at the heart of that United side, who reached the European Cup semi-finals, he was the man England boss Alf Ramsey decided to build his team around for a first World Cup on home soil.

After an opening goalless draw with Uruguay, Charlton gave England's campaign lift-off with a scampering run from the halfway line followed by a trademark strike from distance that was still rising when it crashed into the corner of the Wembley net.

Charlton helped carry the hosts to the semi-finals, where he scored twice as England knocked out Euesbio's Portugal, before he and West Germany's Franz Beckenbauer tussled in the final as England won 4-2 in extra time.

Benfica's Eusebio finished second to Charlton in the Ballon d'Or rankings that year and Bayern Munich's Beckenbauer was third, with Charlton's World Cup performances showcasing not only his knack for a goal, passing range and poise, but also his selflessness. A true team player.

It was a fruitful era for individual success at Old Trafford. Scotland's Denis Law won the Ballon d'Or two years earlier and Northern Ireland's George Best scooped the prize in 1968 as United won the European Cup.

Charlton finished second in successive years after his triumph. But his pivotal role in England's World Cup triumph earns him the runner-up spot on this list.

Kevin Keegan spent three seasons at Hamburg, helping the club to a first German title since the formation of the Bundesliga

Kevin Keegan scored 20 times as Liverpool won the European Cup and English First Division in 1977, and were a whisker away from completing the Treble - losing the FA Cup final to Manchester United. He also finished as Ballon d'Or runner-up to Borussia Monchengladbach's Danish star Allan Simonsen.

Keegan followed Simonsen to Germany by the time the award was announced, joining Cup Winners' Cup holders Hamburg on a lucrative deal, though they were hammered by his former side in the European Super Cup - and Keegan endured a slow start to Bundesliga life that was not helped by an eight-week suspension for punching someone in a friendly during the winter break.

Keegan soon settled but his first season was nothing spectacular. Instead 'Mighty Mouse' made a flying start to the new campaign - one in which Hamburg would become Bundesliga champions for the first time.

He also produced some impressive individual displays for England, even though the Three Lions missed out on that summer's World Cup, scoring against Brazil in a friendly and twice against Denmark in September.

Keegan's form was timely. He arguably deserved to win the Ballon d'Or a year earlier, which perhaps lingered in voters' minds, making 22 of the 26 ballot papers and ranking top on nine to finish above Barcelona's Austrian star Hans Krankl.

Keegan continued to flourish after Christmas to finish with 17 top-flight goals, including 11 in 12 games during the run-in. But it was more than just the goals, with Keegan a scampering, relentlessly creative presence.

The fluid, attacking HSV were in the midst of storming their way to a European Cup final when Keegan was named Europe's best player for a second successive year on Christmas Day 1979, this time being named top by 18 of the 26 voters.

The permed Keegan was the continent's biggest star and a cultural icon, too.

Hamburg would lose the European Cup final to Nottingham Forest at the Bernabeu, Keegan's last game before leaving for Southampton.

But he stands alone as the only British player to win two Ballon d'Or awards - at the time, only Johan Cruyff had matched his feat of going back-to-back - and that, of course, makes him England's greatest recipient.

#353. 17 años y 1 día después Pellegrini vuelve al lugar maldito: Alcorcón

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

El míster del Betis regresa al banquillo de Santo Domingo, desde el cual vivió el Alcorconazo en el partido del Copa ante el Navalcarnero

Casualidades de la vida, caprichos del fútbol o... se puede llamarlo como quiera, pero Manuel Pellegrini regresa a un estadio, Santo Domingo, que no le trae buenos recuerdos. El chileno lo pisó una vez en su vida (27 de octubre de 2009) y fue la noche del famoso Alcorconazo, donde su Madrid perdió 4-0 con los de Juan Antonio Anquela en lo que fue el partido de ida de la Copa del Rey. En el Bernabéu, los blancos cayeron eliminados por los alfareros de Segunda B (ahora Primera RFEF).

Y, justamente, 17 años y 1 día después, Pellegrini regresa al lugar maldito (20 de octubre de 2026) a jugar, otra vez, la Copa del Rey, aunque en esta ocasión ante el Artístico Navalcarnero, equipo de Segunda RFEF. Y es que el estadio de los rojiblancos, el Mariano González se queda muy pequeño para todos los seguidores que quieren disfrutar del choque copero. La decisión de jugar en Alcorcón fue después de saber el rival y ante las limitaciones impuestas por la RFEF de aforo y "para dar cabida a todos nuestros socios, abonados y vecinos", asegura a MARCA el presidente Juan Alarcón.

Alcorcón y Naval siempre tuvieron una gran relación y, por ello, el presidente Juan Alarcón pidió a su vecino alcorconero jugar  en Santo Domingo y no hubo ningún tipo de problemas. Campo cercano y con un aforo que puede cuadrar a los del Naval.

Alarcón recuerda el Alcorconazo, ya que él lo vivió cerca del palco. "Es un partido que no se puede olvidar", y cree que es casualidad lo del sorteo: "Son cosas que pasan. Lo importante es jugar, competir bien y, aunque todos somos conscientes que es muy complicado que un modesto gane a un grande... en el fútbol todo tiene cabida. Ojalá repitamos un Alcorconazo, pero es difícil". Lo que sí quiere el presidente del Naval es que su equipo "deje buen poso con su juego ante uno de los grandes del fútbol español y que todos disfruten con este partido copero".

Manuel Pellegrini llegó a Santo Domingo con su rostro de siempre, serio y abriendo una expedición que contaba con los Raúl, Guti, Metzelder, Diarra, Arbeloa, Van Der Vaart, Benzema, Gago, Marcelo... Según pasaban los minutos e iban cayendo los goles amarillos, la cara de El Ingeniero fue cambiando como de los suplentes y titulares. Sufrió el míster y sufrieron los jugadores y el madridismo. Muchos se frotaban los ojos, mientras que Van Der Vaart, con una educación exquisita, felicitó a los alfareros.

La salida de Pellegrini fue con su rostro serio, pero en esta ocasión también de sufrimiento: se subió al autobús del Madrid, se sentó en el primer asiento, sopló en varias ocasiones, cara pensativa, mirada perdida... y por detrás de él un joven Benzema con unos cascos blancos como 'ajeno' a lo sucedido. Con esa imagen, el bus del Madrid salió del estadio Santo Domingo.

#217. Nigeria: NFF Hails Alex Iwobi On Another Milestone in Premier League

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Nigeria Football Federation (NFF) has celebrated Super Eagles' midfield ace Alexander Iwobi on his new milestone of becoming the joint-second most-capped African football player in the English Premiership, alongside Egyptian icon Mohamed Salah.

Iwobi, who celebrated his 100th cap for Nigeria during this summer's friendly match with Portugal at the Estádio Dr. Magalhães Pessoa in Leiria, has now featured in 328 matches in the English Premier League.

Acting General Secretary of the NFF, Dr Emmanuel Ikpeme MON, congratulated Iwobi on his latest accomplishment.

"The NFF celebrates Alex Iwobi for this new achievement and we wish him even greater glory. We will be very happy to see him become the African with the most appearances in the Premier League in the near future," prayed Dr Ikpeme.

The 30-year-old, who has won silver and two bronze medals with Nigeria at the Africa Cup of Nations, achieved the latest milestone when he featured for his English Premiership club Fulham in their 1-0 win over Coventry City on Saturday.

The game was his 328th appearance in the EPL, taking him joint-second with Salah, and 25 games more to match the all-time record holder, Ivorian Kolo Toure.

Ivorian legend Toure played a total of 353 matches in the EPL - widely regarded as the biggest and most glamorous football league in the world.

In the match with Coventry, the Nigerian superstar had 60 touches on the ball in his 73-minute appearance, during which he made 51 passes, and accomplished 86.3% passing accuracy.

#380. Putellas wins record-equalling third Ballon d'Or

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Alexia Putellas won four trophies with Barcelona last season and 38 over the course of 14 years

Spain midfielder Alexia Putellas has won the Ballon d'Or Feminin for a record-equalling third time, keeping the prize in Spanish hands for a sixth consecutive year.

Putellas, 32, captained Barcelona to Champions League glory as part of a quadruple in 2025-26, before joining Women's Super League (WSL) side London City Lionesses in July.

She joins Spain team-mate Aitana Bonmati on three Ballon d'Or Feminin crowns - her former Barcelona colleague won the award in 2023, 2024 and 2025 after Putellas did so in 2021 and 2022.

The past six Ballons d'Or have been won by the Spanish pair, with Norway's Ada Hegerberg (2018) and Megan Rapinoe of the United States (2019) the only non-Spaniards to claim the prize.

Putellas' latest victory caps a remarkable return to the pinnacle of world football after suffering a career-threatening anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) injury in 2022.

"All the boys and girls around the world - if you love football and if you want to play football, play football. It doesn't matter if you're a boy or if you're a girl," Putellas said.

"Doesn't matter where you come from. Don't listen if someone tells you, you can't do it. Believe in yourself."

She collected the most votes from an international panel of journalists across Fifa's top-50 ranked countries, with Barcelona striker Ewa Pajor coming second and Manchester City forward Khadija Shaw third.

Meanwhile, England captain Harry Kane won the men's Ballon d'Or, becoming the first English player to do so since Michael Owen in 2001.

While Barcelona are always overwhelming favourites to win the three competitions on offer in Spain, Putellas' performances last season showed that she is still among the world's elite.

Nicknamed 'La Reina' - meaning the queen - Putellas scored 21 goals and provided 13 assists in all competitions, ranking her third and second respectively among players across Europe's top five leagues.

Seven of those goals came in the Women's Champions League as Barcelona claimed a fourth continental title in six seasons.

Only Pernille Harder (eight), Alessia Russo (nine) and Ewa Pajor (11) scored more, all of whom are strikers by trade.

But Putellas' contributions to Barcelona's attacking play went far beyond scoring goals herself.

In Liga F - which Barcelona won for a seventh consecutive season - she finished joint-sixth for assists (seven) and fourth for open-play chances created (40).

Across Europe's top five leagues, only Claudia Pina (199), Vicky Lopez (183) and Georgia Stanway (168) recorded more sequence involvements ending in an open-play shot than Putellas (151).

In all competitions, she created more chances (89, 19 of which were big chances) and completed more passes ending in the final third (965) than any other player in Liga F, the Champions League, Copa de la Reina and Supercopa de Espana.

Only Bayern Munich's Klara Buhl created as many chances as Putellas in the Champions League last season (36), the most by a player in a single edition of the competition since Caroline Graham Hansen in 2021-22 (38).

Putellas was born and grew up in the Catalan municipality of Mollet del Valles, about 12 miles north-east of Barcelona.

She began playing football in the street, and soon became a Barcelona fan and a regular attendee at Camp Nou.

With matches always on the TV at home, former Spain and Barcelona star Andres Iniesta and Brazilian duo Rivaldo and Ronaldinho quickly became her idols.

After attending a summer football camp run by Xavi and playing for local side CE Sabadell, Putellas spent a year at La Masia, Barcelona's famous youth academy.

Spells at Espanyol and Levante followed the disbandment of her girls team, before she eventually returned to Barca in 2012.

Cue the start of a 14-year career with her childhood club during which she won 38 trophies, including 10 league titles and four European cups.

But after winning the Ballon d'Or for the first time in 2021, one of the toughest moments of Putellas' career came when she suffered an ACL injury on the eve of Euro 2022, ruling her out of the tournament.

Though she retained the Ballon d'Or later that year, it took a while for her to return to her best level. Playing only a bit-part role in Spain's triumphant 2023 World Cup campaign would have stung.

After scoring in the 2024 Champions League final and improving further the following season, in 2025-26 she shouldered much of Barcelona's creative burden as Aitana Bonmati missed five months through injury and ended the season a quadruple winner.

If that was not enough, the midfielder scored twice in a 4-0 thrashing of England in June that secured Spain's place at the 2027 World Cup at the Lionesses' expense.

With her contract at Barcelona expiring and keen for a fresh challenge, Putellas made the decision to leave her childhood club. She, and her fans, were distraught.

She had several offers from England, Mexico and the United States, but joined the ambitious project of US billionaire Michelle Kang at London City Lionesses in July.

"I'm just always looking forward to better things and that's why I'm here too, because I always need to be moving as a person and a player," Putellas told BBC's Women's Football Weekly podcast in August.

With three goals and one assist in seven WSL games for her new club, it is clear Putellas is still deserving of her nickname.

#332. Kane ends England's 25-year Ballon d'Or wait - how he bucked the trend

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Harry Kane broke Bayern Munich's goalscoring record for a season in his third campaign at the club

Harry Kane has become the first English player to win the Ballon d'Or since Michael Owen in 2001 after being crowned the world's best player at a ceremony in London.

The 33-year-old Bayern Munich striker is the fifth Englishman to win in total, with Stanley Matthews (1956), Bobby Charlton (1966) and Kevin Keegan (1978 and 1979) the others.

Denis Law of Scotland (1964) and Northern Ireland's George Best (1968) are the other winners from the United Kingdom.

Real Madrid and France forward Kylian Mbappe finished second, while Barcelona and Spain attacker Lamine Yamal completed the top three.

"Not many people would have thought it, and maybe even I wouldn't have thought I'd be this far," Kane told Dazn.

"I had to go through many ups and downs, especially early in my career. I was never the person that people thought was going to be the superstar like some of the guys now.

"I had to bide my time and work hard, a lot of times it was me against my own mind and I had to come through it, I had to show self-belief, determination.

"I think that grounded me even when I did make it at the highest level to keep improving and keep getting better."

England manager Thomas Tuchel said he was "delighted" to see Kane win the award as he hailed the forward's success as a "huge honour".

Prince William, meanwhile, said the former Tottenham Hotspur player was a "brilliant captain" and "remarkable player".

Kane scored 61 goals in 51 club matches last season, the most a Bayern player has ever struck in one campaign. Including England matches, it was 73 goals - plus eight assists - in 65 matches.

Only one player at a club in Europe's top five leagues has ever scored more goals in a season.

But he wins it despite not lifting either of football's biggest prizes last season, having lost in both the World Cup and Champions League semi-finals.

Remarkably the Three Lions captain becomes the first Bayern player to win the Ballon d'Or since Karl-Heinz Rummenigge in 1981.

To celebrate Kane's success, the Wembley arches were lit up in gold on Monday night.

BBC Sport looks at why Kane won it and how unusual it is to do so without securing one of those major trophies.

In terms of trophies, Kane may have 'only' lifted the German double last season.

But that more than doubled his career trophy total - having also won the Bundesliga the season before.

But in terms of individual output Kane - whose previous best Ballon d'Or finish was 10th - was unmatched last season.

His 61 goals in all competitions for Bayern was 19 more than any other player in Europe's top five leagues managed.

In fact it was the most anyone has recorded since Cristiano Ronaldo also notched 61 for Real Madrid 11 years ago.

He was the Bundesliga's top scorer with 36 goals - 17 clear of anyone else. In the Champions League he scored 14 for the beaten semi-finalists.

In the German Cup he scored 10 goals, including a hat-trick in the 3-0 win over Stuttgart in the final, and he also netted once in the German Super Cup.

No player has ever scored so many goals in a season for Bayern, a club who have had legendary goalscorers like Gerd Muller, Rummenigge and more recently Robert Lewandowski in their ranks.

Kane's 12 goals for England last season - including six at the World Cup - take him up to a total of 73.

Only one player in history from Europe's top leagues has scored more in a season, Lionel Messi's 82 for Barcelona and Argentina in 2011-12.

Next on the list are Muller (72 in 1972-73), Cristiano Ronaldo (66 in both 2011-12 and 2014-15), former Brazil striker Ronaldo (66 in 1996-97), Erling Haaland (64 in 2022-23) and Luis Suarez (64 in 2015-16).

Former England striker Alan Shearer recently told BBC Sport: "The winner of the Ballon d'Or should be Harry Kane.

"His numbers for the last 12 to 18 months have been absolutely sensational for Bayern and for England. For that reason, he should get it."

Another ex-Three Lions player Danny Murphy added: "His goalscoring has been relentless, brilliant. He's had one of the best seasons of his career and it'll be a great reward for him if he gets the trophy.

"He's been brilliant at Bayern Munich since he's been there, adapted really well and he's been the best centre forward in the world for the last 12 months."

The Ballon d'Or was only available to European players from its formation in 1956 until 1995 when it was opened up to everyone.

Since then in four of the seven World Cup years, the Ballon d'Or has been awarded to a world champion - including Messi in 2023 (as the 2022 World Cup was in the winter).

However, that was the first time since 2006 it had happened, with Messi, Cristiano Ronaldo and Luka Modric all winning since then despite not winning the World Cup that year.

This is only the fifth time in the past 20 years that the Ballon d'Or has not gone to a player who has won either the World Cup, the Champions League or a European Championship/Copa America - with Kane joining Messi (three times) and Ronaldo.

Lamine Yamal, who won the World Cup with Spain, had been one of the favourites but lost out to Kane.

Last year, winning the Champions League helped PSG and France's Ousmane Dembele to the crown.

Kane may have just won the Ballon d'Or but some are already wondering if he is in a good place to retain it next year.

The striker has scored 10 goals in his opening 12 games for Bayern this season.

Throw in six goals in four Nations League games for England and that is a total of 16 in 16 matches so far.

Bayern are unbeaten in the Bundesliga and Champions League this season.

If they were to win a Treble - and Kane keeps scoring at about a goal a game - it would be tough to look past him, especially in a season with no World Cup, European Championship or Copa America.

The last player other than Messi or Ronaldo to win it two years in a row was Marco van Basten in 1988 and 1989.

However, Lamine Yamal will be hoping to make it to the top, having now finished second and third over the past two years.

He has started the season in electric form, with 10 goals and 10 assists in 13 games for Barcelona in La Liga and the Champions League.

And Barca are currently favourites to win the Champions League, which could ultimately end up being a deciding factor.

He would be the youngest player to win the Ballon d'Or - aged 20 - if he does lift it next year.

Real Madrid's Mbappe will also fancy his chances of winning for the first time, with 14 goals in 13 appearances for club and country.

The youngest winner remains Brazilian Ronaldo, who was 21 when he won in 1997.

Arsenal, who have never had a Ballon d'Or winner, and Paris St-Germain are two of the other frontrunners to win the Champions League.

#257. Damning data: stats that show Michael Carrick’s luck was bound to run out | Andrew Beasley

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Favourable conditions and the rub of the green were behind Manchester United’s successful finish to last season

Manchester United have been the third‑best team in the Premier League with Michael Carrick as their head coach. It is some effort for his job to be so under threat when only Arsenal (57 points) and Manchester City (56) have been more productive than United (46) during the 45-year-old’s tenure.

The tally obscures pertinent facts. United topping the Premier League during his 17‑match interim spell last season is irrelevant when they sit 16th with no win in eight games in all competitions this season. Losing narrowly against City is one thing, but three-goal defeats by Como and Bournemouth will surely spell managerial change.

Carrick can point to a degree of misfortune this season. Conceding stoppage-time equalisers to Everton and Tottenham was costly, although they also required an 89th-minute goal to avoid losing at Fulham, who were winless at the time.

Opta’s expected points model suggests United have earned 3.8 points fewer than their underlying statistical performances merit in 2026-27. Only three clubs are further adrift of where they can reasonably expect to be.

Equally, United’s expected position of 10th is hardly befitting a club whose net transfer spend this summer topped all those outside the Premier League bar Real Madrid, even before we consider their annual revenue and illustrious history. Their par level remains uninspiring.

If they have been a touch unlucky with results, it was almost an inevitable outcome. United outperformed their expected points by 12.7 in Carrick’s share of 2025-26, 7.6 more than any other club. Such fortune was never going to last, as any back office paying the remotest attention to data would know. Poor recruitment in the summer increased the likelihood of a slump.

The run of results appeared as unsustainable as it has proven. Eight of United’s 12 league wins under Carrick last season were by one goal. In that time, only Napoli (nine) had more such victories in Europe’s big five leagues. The expected goal margin was no more than 0.6 in United’s favour in five of those eight victories and they lost the xG by about 0.6 in their draws at West Ham and Sunderland. A lot of coin-flips went in their favour.

United’s woes are not simply the result of luck turning against them. They are incapable of breaking down teams when dominating the ball. Something that seemed insurmountable for Ole Gunnar Solskjær has also hampered Carrick. Meet the new overpromoted club legend, same as the old overpromoted club legend.

In Carrick’s first game, United won a Manchester derby despite having 32% possession. They were similarly ball-averse in victories against Liverpool (38%), Chelsea (40%) and Arsenal (43%) last season. Yet they have drawn blanks against Hull and Bournemouth in 2026-27 despite having 72% and 63% possession respectively. They were also unable to break down City when having a man advantage for three-quarters of the contest and 55% possession.

United lead the division for shots, but no team have had a higher proportion of their efforts from outside the penalty area, which explains why their non‑penalty expected goals per shot average is the second‑worst in the Premier League.
