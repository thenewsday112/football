#4. 'He could be happier' - why is Estevao becoming Chelsea's forgotten man?

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Brazilian forward Estevao has made just two starts across all competitions this season

The last time Chelsea beat Liverpool, Estevao was the talk of the Premier League.

The Brazilian was still in his first couple of months at Stamford Bridge when his name hit the headlines after he scored the winner against the Premier League champions with practically the final kick of the match last October.

Fast forward 12 months, and Estevao is beginning to attract attention for all the wrong reasons as he struggles to force his way into Xabi Alonso's side.

The 19-year-old, who joined Chelsea from Palmeiras last summer, was conspicuous by his absence on Wednesday night as Chelsea were knocked out of the Carabao Cup following a penalty shootout loss to Liverpool at Anfield.

The Blues had taken the lead in the first half through Morgan Rogers, but they failed to kick on after Cody Gakpo quickly restored parity for the hosts.

Most concerning for Estevao, though, was that as both teams pushed for a winner in the second half, Alonso never appeared to consider the forward.

The clearest indication of just how low Estevao sits in Alonso's pecking order came midway through the second half, when Alonso made a triple substitution in an attempt to turn the game in his side's favour.

One of the three changes was Emanuel Emegha, who had yet to feature for the Blues since his summer move from Strasbourg.

Just moments after that triple change, which brought Chelsea's total number of substitutions to five, Estevao could be seen ending his warm-up and retreating to the bench.

It was another night of no action for the Brazilian.

Sadly for Estevao, Wednesday night's situation at Anfield was nothing new for him.

The Brazilian has failed to make the starting XI in any of the eight Premier League matches since Alonso took charge of the Blues in the summer.

His total minutes for the campaign so far stand at an underwhelming 172, with 125 of those coming in the victories over Luton Town and Leeds United in the earlier rounds of the EFL Cup.

Yet even in the thrilling 6-3 win against Daniel Farke's side, which remains one of only two games he has started this campaign, he was withdrawn at half-time with Chelsea trailing 1-0.

After the fourth-round defeat by Liverpool, Alonso defended his decision not to play Estevao, hinting that the forward is still coming to grips with the physical demands of English football.

"We started with Pedro [Neto], Morgan [Rogers] and Danny Welbeck, and the game had very intense moments," said Alonso.

"We knew it was going to be physical, and Malo [Gusto] had cramps, so we were a bit forced with substitutions. The season is going to be very long, and there will be moments for everyone."

When asked directly about the Brazilian, Alonso added: "He could be happier. He wants to play. I'm sure Estevao will have his moment, it is my responsibility.

"We know the game in England is really intense, and you need to be up for it. Estevao is in his second season. The season is going to be long."

Neto scored both goals in Chelsea's 2-0 win over Everton this month

One of the main reasons behind Estevao's exile has been the form of Pedro Neto.

It was only a couple of months ago that Portugal international Neto looked destined to leave Chelsea, only two years after joining from Wolverhampton Wanderers.

The 26-year-old was linked with a move to Saudi Pro League side Al-Ahli, but he ultimately remained at Stamford Bridge.

That decision appears to have been influenced by Alonso, who has underlined his faith in Neto by handing him seven starts in eight Premier League matches this season.

His success out on the right - which also happens to be Estevao's favoured position - has meant opportunities have been few and far between for the Brazilian.

He has primarily been deployed as a right-sided wing-back in Alonso's 3-4-2-1 system, with his Premier League experience and greater defensive solidity compared to Estevao seemingly winning over the Spaniard.

After an impressive start to the campaign, in which he has scored four goals in 11 matches, Neto was rewarded with a new six-year deal at Stamford Bridge.

"He's a player who is a little bit of a victim of the system," former Reading midfielder Jobi McAnuff told Sky Sports.

"Alonso tends to play Neto in that hybrid wing-back role, expecting him to get high up the pitch as well. Maybe he doesn't feel that suits Estevao, and I don't feel it does either."

One area that has not been a problem for the Blues this season is attack, with the forward positions firmly in the hands of Joao Pedro, Cole Palmer and Rogers, who has made a spectacular start to life at Stamford Bridge following his Â£117m move from Aston Villa.

Against Liverpool, though, Estevao couldn't even get a look-in despite Chelsea being without the suspended Palmer and the injured Joao Pedro.

"How Estevao has not got on the pitch tonight, I do not know," said former Crystal Palace forward Clinton Morrison.

"He must be thinking, 'What do I have to do? We have injuries to two of the star players up front, and I'm still not getting on the pitch'."

Estevao will be hoping he can follow in the footsteps of Neto and revive his Stamford Bridge career.

#23. European football: Bayern ease to Cup victory after Kompany leaves hospital

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Kane gets guard of honour before 6-1 win over Magdeburg

Serie A: Inter sink Venezia, Milan lose at home to Bologna

Bayern Munich sailed to a 6-1 win over second-tier Magdeburg in the DFB-Pokal after their head coach, Vincent Kompany, was discharged from hospital earlier on Wednesday.

Kompany’s assistant, Aaron Danks, was in the dugout for Bayern for the last-32 tie, while Harry Kane was rested. The Ballon d’Or winner received a guard of honour from his teammates before kick-off in front of 75,000 fans at the Allianz Arena.

Bayern had no difficulty without their star striker, with Michael Olise opening the scoring and adding a second-half penalty. Ismael Saibari, Alphonso Davies, Nathaniel Brown and teenager David Santos Daiber also got on the scoresheet, with Mateusz Zukowski striking for the visitors.

The German Cup holders received good news pre-match, with Kompany able to return home five days after collapsing at training on Friday. The Bayern Munich sporting director, Max Eberl, revealed that the Belgian had been suffering from meningeal irritation.

“We have identified the cause: it is a case of meningeal irritation, which is what triggered the incident last Friday,” Eberl said. “He has now been allowed to go home, where he can continue his rehabilitation … [which] means rest and medication. I am very, very confident that he will be back on the touchline at 100% very soon.”

Olise tapped in from an excellent Saibari assist after 10 minutes but the unfancied visitors levelled five minutes later through Zukowski. Olise then set up Saibari midway through the opening half and added a third from the spot early in the second half before smiling and pointing to Kane, Bayern’s regular penalty taker, on the bench.

The teenage forward Lennart Karl set up goals for Davies and Brown, before Santos Daiber added more gloss to the victory with a stoppage-time goal. “We’re also winning for him of course,” Brown said of Kompany. “It’s really good that he’s back home now. He should get some rest, but hopefully we’ll see him again soon.”

“We’re very pleased that [Vincent] is getting better,” said Danks, who will continue to lead the team while Kompany recovers. “But he needs to rest, spend time with his family and continue to get better. He’s doing better and is on the road to recovery. We’ll simply try to do our best and prepare for the game on Saturday.”

Elsewhere, Bayer Leverkusen came from two goals down to win 5-2 against 2. Bundesliga promotion-chasers Nuremberg, with defender Edmond Tapsoba netting twice and Patrik Schick, Jonas Hofmann and Lucas Vázquez also on target.

Deniz Undav’s 90th-minute strike earned Stuttgart a 1-0 win over 10-man Paderborn, while Freiburg also won in stoppage time. The Japan forward Keisuke Goto scored twice in the final 15 minutes to turn a 2-1 deficit into a 3-2 victory over Wolfsburg.

Second-tier Dynamo Dresden upset Schalke 2-0, and Holstein Kiel eliminated Hoffenheim 4-3 on penalties after a 1-1 draw to reach the last 16.

There was a huge upset across the border in Poland, as defending league champions Lech Poznan were beaten 1-0 by fourth-tier Siarka Tarnobrzeg in a Cup tie that kicked off at 12.30pm on Wednesday.

Lech held Sunderland to a 1-1 draw in the Europa League last week and beat Legia Warsaw on Sunday to go top of the Polish top-flight – but they were brought down to earth by Arkadiusz Kasia’s second-half header.

Internazionale returned to the Serie A summit with a nervy 2-0 win at bottom side Venezia, while Bologna won 4-2 at San Siro to inflict a first league defeat of the season on Milan.

Champions Inter drew level with Lazio on 25 points at the top before a hotly anticipated Milan derby on Saturday. Ruben Amorim’s side lost their unbeaten streak after Alphadjo Cisse’s double was not enough to avoid defeat, as Samuel Mbangula’s hat-trick sealed a 4-2 win for the visitors.

Venezia have picked up just three points all season, but earned them with a shock win over Napoli on new coach Davide Nicola’s debut. Another shock result looked possible on Wednesday until they were undone by two late own goals, with Matías Moreno and Redouane Halhal diverting crosses into their own net.

At San Siro, a lengthy injury list restricted Amorim’s options, and the Milan coach also left Christian Pulisic and Luka Modric on the bench. Mbangula, making his full debut for Bologna, swept home the opener inside eight minutes, but the visitors were soon pegged back when an Arthur Theate error gifted Cissé a simple finish.

The teenager struck again in the 27th minute, volleying home a Fikayo Tomori cross, but the game was turned on its head once again in the space of two second-half minutes. Davide Bartesaghi turned a free-kick into his own net before Mbangula struck again just after the hour mark. The 22-year-old sealed victory four minutes from time, beating Mike Maignan after a breakaway.

Juventus’s shaky start continued with a 2-0 defeat away to Genoa. Kingsley Ehizibue struck early before Tommaso Baldanzi doubled Genoa’s lead in the 28th minute. Luciano Spalletti’s inconsistent side have not won back-to-back league games since the opening two weeks of the season, and face Napoli next.

With head coach Massimiliano Allegri suspended after being sent off during the 3-3 draw with Roma, Napoli enjoyed their biggest win of the season, winning 4-1 at Monza to go sixth in the table, above Juve on goal difference.

Rasmus Højlund’s early opener was cancelled out by Gustavo Varela’s header just before the break, but Napoli pulled away in the second half as Matteo Politano, Rafa Marín and substitute Kevin De Bruyne all scored. Elsewhere, Adrián Bernabé saw his penalty saved as struggling Parma and Udinese played out a 0-0 draw.

All 24 Copa del Rey matches scheduled for Wednesday evening have been postponed as a mark of respect following the devastating rail incident in Spain.

A high-speed train crashed into the rear of another train on Wednesday morning and derailed two cars in the province of Toledo, with at least 68 people killed.

La Liga clubs Alavés, Celta Vigo, Levante, Real Betis and Valencia were due to get their domestic cup campaigns under way, but all of Wednesday’s fixtures will now be played at later dates.

Real Madrid, Barcelona and Atlético Madrid are not due to enter until the last 32 in December, but all three issued statements expressing sadness and deepest condolences.

Chelsea have won two and drawn one of their opening three league phase games in the Champions League

Chelsea's 2-0 victory over Roma in the Women's Champions League was yet another demonstration they can win in different ways.

That followed a dream unbeaten season in which the Blues wrapped up a domestic treble in Bompastor's first year in charge.

Having previously won six successive WSL titles, a blip was inevitable and their transition period highlighted flaws in the new era under their French boss.

There was still optimism in this campaign and, while Bompastor expected it would take more time, Chelsea have started impressively.

They are unbeaten in all competitions, top of the Women's Super League with Manchester City after seven games, and have won two and drawn one of their opening three Champions League phase matches.

"I'm really pleased with the results for sure, but not only the results, I think the different performances," said Bompastor earlier this week.

"Right now, the way we are performing gives us a really good advantage and a big chance to win games."

"I'm really pleased with the solid performances in terms of the defensive aspects. We have played three games and we haven't conceded," added Bompastor.

"When you want to go far in competitions, it's good to have this solid base. With the quality we have in the team, I'm confident we will always create opportunities."

She admitted the Champions League was "a different level" to what they had already overcome in the WSL and Bompastor has rotated her squad effectively to cope in both competitions.

They seem to have sorted out their defensive leaks that were so evident last season and look more clinical going forward.

Full-back Carpenter has four goals in all competitions, England forward Lauren James is in scintillating form and young midfielder Lexi Potter is developing a flourishing partnership with Lionesses star Keira Walsh.

They look unpredictable, being able to adapt shape in a back three or a back four, and can go direct with the aid of goalkeeper Hannah Hampton's distribution.

They had to grind out their win over Roma in a way that former boss Emma Hayes' sides used to do - proof in itself they have learned from the previous campaign.

"Even when we opened the competition against Austria Vienna, it was a tough game because they were brave and exposed us to some challenges," said Bompastor.

"We found a way to win, but obviously we want to control every game as much as we can, no matter what competition it is.

"Tonight and on Sunday we rotated the team a little bit. We find different ways to impose ourselves in the game and to score goals. That's good."

Manchester City won the WSL title impressively last season but externally, there was an expectation Chelsea would bounce back.

Bompastor was more cautious, insisting they were still transitioning after the departures of big-name players including Sam Kerr, Millie Bright and Guro Reiten.

She said: "You need to consider the fact that Chelsea had the same coach for 12 years. It takes a while for players to adjust to a new coach, a new way of playing and a philosophy.

"Even if you have really good players, it doesn't just go like this. There are a lot of things we are doing behind the scenes for us to stay successful.

"Manchester City ran away with the league in the end, but we were there or thereabouts. I feel this time the team's just gone up another level."

"For me, the mental aspects and cultural aspects are just as important," admitted Bompastor.

"We probably needed to rebuild a little bit of that going into this new season. We reflected a lot on how we wanted to set up the process for that.

"Right now, we are in a good place. When you are able to get good results, it's not only about the tactical aspects. It also has to be about mentality, culture and values.
