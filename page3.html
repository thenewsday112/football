#28. Namibia: Kavango West Elects New Football Leadership

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

- The Kavango West Regional Football Association (KWRFA) has elected new leadership to oversee football activities in the region following a successful Elective Annual General Meeting (AGM) in Nkurenkuru last Saturday.

The uncontested one-slate elections saw Egidius Nambara elected as chairperson, with Johannes Rugharo as his deputy.

Nambara and Rugharo return to the association's Executive Committee (EXCO) after serving on the outgoing EXCO, where Nambara served as vice-chairperson to Ferdinand Hausiku, and Rugharo as competition coordinator.

The other EXCO members include Michael Haingura, Gerhard Huumbwa, Matheus Mukuve, Joseph Shigwedha and Ludwina Katura.

Follow us on WhatsApp | LinkedIn for the latest headlines

The new team will be at the helm of football activities in the region for four years.

Delivering his acceptance speech, Nambara, while acknowledging that the task entrusted to them as the new leadership is not an easy one, called for unity with everyone in the football fraternity in the region.

"We can only develop football when we remain united, support one another and work together," he said.

He expressed his appreciation to the outgoing leadership and commended their work, describing it as unwavering.

"It was not an easy journey. It was rough at times, with names being called and accusations of misappropriation of funds. It was not easy, but you had to be strong," Nambara said.

He thus encouraged his fellow EXCO members not to take things personally, saying that members affiliated to the association will ask when things are not working out well.

The outgoing chairperson, Ferdinand Hausiku, on his part, as he handed over power, said that the journey in their leadership has not been easy, as they have sometimes been labelled.

"But as someone who has accepted to be in that position, you take heart, you breathe in and out, and then see how you can move forward," Hausiku said.

He, however, said that they have worked tirelessly to ensure that the region is well catered for, and added that they are proud of what the region has achieved under their leadership.

He referenced unity between the association and affiliated member clubs, and the region's dominant performance at the renowned youth sports competition, the Namibian Newspaper Cup, since 2023, when the region first claimed bragging rights in the football competition.

#3. Liverpool beat Chelsea on penalties yet again to secure Carabao Cup progress

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Chelsea must have been resigned to their fate as soon as the shootout arrived at Anfield. Four times they had faced Liverpool on penalties previously and four times they had lost. Number five was confirmed when Wesley Fofana’s wild effort sailed high into the Kop and sent Andoni Iraola’s side into a Carabao Cup quarter-final against his old club Bournemouth.

Fofana was not the only culprit as Chelsea’s catalogue of penalty misery against Liverpool gained a new chapter alongside the 2007 Champions League semi-final, the 2019 Uefa Super Cup final and the FA and League Cup finals of 2022.

Emmanuel Emegha had the chance to win it for Xabi Alonso’s side in sudden death but was denied superbly by Giorgi Mamardashvili. Liverpool fluffed their lines with victory in sight too, Kostas Tsimikas blazing over and ruining the chance to reprise his match-winning role at Wembley four years ago. Ryan Gravenberch and Valentin Barco also failed to convert.

It fell to Joe Gomez of all people to edge Liverpool ahead with the first shootout penalty of his Anfield career. The long-serving defender, who has never scored for Liverpool, found the top corner of Mike Penders’s goal in convincing style. Fofana could not repeat the trick and finally, after Morgan Rogers and Cody Gakpo had both scored penalties in normal time, the two adversaries were separated. #17. Rulli’s heroics earn Manchester City shootout success against Brighton

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

After Jérémy Doku’s second-half substitute appearance transformed Manchester City into a high-octane proposition, the smart money was on the momentum continuing into a victorious penalty shootout.

So it proved as Enzo Maresca’s men triumphed 4-3, Gerónimo Rulli the hero: his save from Lewis Dunk’s fifth spot-kick was decisive, the Argentinean also having repelled Yasin Ayari’s fourth effort.

While Jason Steele kept out Mateo Kovacic’s attempt, City progressed after Brighton twice took the lead – via Malick Yalcouyé and Georginio Rutter – before being pegged back by Kovacic and Vitor Reis.

Maresca was left beaming: “Since I’m here we never practised penalties,” said the City manager. “It always happens that the one that scored in the 90 minutes [Kovacic] misses the penalty. I am very happy.”

Of Doku, he said: “He made an impact. This is the reason they are there. Jérémy and the rest – to try to do their best, if they start from the beginning or if they come in from the bench try to make an impact.”

The tie might have gone either way, so the lottery of penalties felt apt to decide who went through before Saturday’s rematch back here in the league. City will now play League One Bradford in the quarter-finals.

“I watched all [Brighton’s] Premier League games and since the first day they always go front foot, man to man, so they’re going to be exactly the same on Saturday,” Maresca said.

His opposite number spoke of vengeance. “We lost in a shootout. We had enough chances to win the game – they had the luck today. We have to reset. We have a great chance on Saturday to take revenge,” said Fabian Hürzeler.

Ties will take place during the week commencing 14 December

Brighton will stay up in the area. “Not sleeping in your own bed is not an advantage,” said the manager. “But we’ll go there with full energy and ready to go on Saturday.”

When Yalcouyé opened the scoring there was the proverbial hint of offside about the forward as his shot pinged off Rulli and in.

With no video assistant referee in the Carabao Cup the goal stood, though it was soon cancelled out by Kovacic: Phil Foden fed him and, from the edge of the penalty area, he threaded the ball beyond Steele low into the left corner.

For the second half Maresca introduced Rayan Aït-Nouri and Ryan McAidoo for Ayyoub Bouaddi and Floyd Samba. Instantly, though, City conceded: Brighton slipped through the lines with ease, and Jaouen Hadjam’s cross was slid in by Rutter.

The forward’s grin went wide in celebration and the Seagulls were heading through, while City were going out. Aït-Nouri, skipping down the left, tried to do something about this, but his ball over was aimless.

At least there was pace in this foray, which those in blue sometimes lacked. But, also, Maresca’s team had become sloppy – a long-range Rayan Cherki pass that ended in Steele’s gloves was emblematic.

Olivier Boscagli’s corner that dipped viciously close to Rulli’s goalline was far more dangerous, so just after the hour Maresca introduced a first big gun – Doku, for Allan – this coming after Rutter ran in and went close to a second, underlining Brighton’s ascendancy.

When Doku played Aït-Nouri in through the left, it was a rare City attack and showed the value of having the Belgian now able to affect the game.

At this point, though, the decision to allow his star quintet the night off was backfiring for Maresca. City lacked control and were toothless. Doku, again, cut through Brighton but there were no runners to prosper from his delivery infield.

Hürzeler’s men tried to squeeze time and snaffle the ball whenever City attempted to move them about, but then came a first passage in which City camped in Brighton territory. Suddenly, those in green were having to answer questions. Foden’s burst that drew a free-kick from Rutter near the 18-yard line presaged one they could not answer. Cherki blasted at goal, Reis stuck out a boot, Steele was wrongfooted, and it was 2-2. Doku had lit up City, his presence terrorising Brighton’s defence and turning the tie.

“They are in a brilliant moment, scoring goals, many goals every game and winning, so we knew it was a tough game in terms of defending in the right way,” Maresca said. “We could have probably done something better defensively, but we started just three months ago [together]. The game was chaotic but we controlled it quite good.”
